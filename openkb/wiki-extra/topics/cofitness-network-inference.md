# Cofitness Network Inference

Cofitness network inference is the practice of using correlated fitness profiles across large-scale mutant screens — most often genome-wide fitness data collected with [[entities/random-barcode-transposon-sequencing]] and cataloged in the [[entities/fitness-browser]] — to hypothesize functional relationships between genes. Because two genes whose loss produces similar fitness costs and benefits across many growth conditions are inferred to share a "fitness program," cofitness has become one of the corpus's primary tools for annotating genes that resist classification by sequence homology alone. This topic sits at the intersection of high-throughput genetics, network biology, and comparative genomics, and the corpus devotes substantial effort to both exploiting cofitness for annotation and stress-testing what the correlation actually means.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Cofitness is a robust, scalable signal at multiple structural levels.** At the pairwise level, the AMR-focused analysis of 28 organisms and 801 AMR genes with fitness data found that 769 genes (96%) had at least one extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3, generating support networks averaging 233 genes at that threshold and shrinking to 71 genes at |r| > 0.5. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] At the module level, independent-component analysis ([[entities/independent-component-analysis]]) of fitness data across 32 organisms recovered 1,116 stable modules, 94.2% of which showed significantly elevated within-module cofitness (Mann–Whitney U, p < 0.05). [src: fitness_modules] Conserved module families extend this structure across taxa: 156 families span at least two organisms, 28 span five or more, and one spans 21 organisms, with 93% carrying a consensus functional label. [src: fitness_modules] Full technical grounding is in [[concepts/cofitness-networks]].

**Annotation quality, not just biology, determines how much signal is visible.** The AMR support-network study found that legacy SEED annotations produced zero significant enrichment results across 280 tests, while [[entities/interproscan]] GO annotations produced 35 significant results across 3,193 tests and raised cross-organism Jaccard similarity from 0.069 to 0.207 (within-mechanism) and 0.249 to 0.375 (cross-mechanism). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] The pan-bacterial module study reached a parallel conclusion from a different angle: adding PFam domains and relaxing the enrichment-overlap threshold from 3 to 2 raised the module annotation rate from 8% to 80% (92 to 890 modules), a 7.6-fold increase in function predictions, while KEGG KOs proved too gene-specific for module-level enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] This is the same annotation-coverage problem documented in [[concepts/annotation-gap]], and it means cofitness-based function calls are only as good as the annotation layer used to interpret them.

**Cofitness recovers real pathway structure and proposes concrete new gene assignments.** In the ADP1 aromatic-catabolism network, cofitness reassigned 16 of 23 previously "Other/Unknown" quinate-pathway genes to a subsystem with medium-or-high confidence, recovered the miscategorized gene pcaC, and flagged ACIAD3137 and ACIAD2176 as candidate Complex I accessory factors based on r > 0.98 correlations with known Complex I genes — explicitly labeled as phenotypic-correlation hypotheses, not physical-association evidence. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] At pan-bacterial scale, module-based inference generated 6,691 function predictions for hypothetical proteins across 32 organisms, of which 2,455 (37%) were reinforced by conserved module families. [src: fitness_modules] See [[concepts/cofitness-networks]] and [[concepts/annotation-gap]] for the surrounding argument about what these predictions can and cannot claim.

**Organism-specific network architecture is a more defensible signal than category-level enrichment.** Different AMR mechanisms within the same organism shared more GO support terms (mean Jaccard 0.375) than the same mechanism did across organisms (mean Jaccard 0.207; MWU p = 4.3×10⁻¹³). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] A conserved functional core — transmembrane transport, signal transduction, transcription regulation, phosphorelay signaling — recurs across organisms, while flagellar motility and amino-acid biosynthesis form a weaker second tier, and no GO term survives FDR correction as mechanism-specific. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] AMR genes also sit in larger ICA modules than non-AMR genes (median 46 vs 27 genes, MWU p = 1.7×10⁻⁸), with 208 of 209 AMR module assignments falling into cross-organism conserved families. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

**Cofitness weakly, and unevenly, predicts natural gene co-inheritance.** Testing whether lab-measured cofitness predicts pangenome co-occurrence across nine organisms, [[concepts/gene-co-inheritance]] found an aggregate delta phi of only +0.003 (0.092 vs 0.089 mean phi, Mann–Whitney p = 1.66×10⁻²⁹) across 2,253,491 cofit pairs versus 22,534,910 random pairs. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] The effect was directionally positive in 7 of 9 organisms but the across-organism Wilcoxon signed-rank test was not significant (W = 9, p = 0.13). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Multi-gene ICA modules carried a substantially stronger signal (delta phi = +0.053 across 195 modules; accessory modules alone reached +0.108), suggesting coordinated modules — not isolated pairs — are the more meaningful co-inheritance unit. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's central unresolved question is whether cofitness reflects genuine mechanistic coupling or **[[concepts/shared-dispensability]]** — genes covarying merely because they are similarly unneeded under standard shaken-liquid, rich-media assay conditions. The AMR network's enriched terms (flagellar motility, histidine and tryptophan biosynthesis) are exactly the functions expected to be dispensable in that assay context, and the conservation-matched permutation did not match on mean fitness level, so it cannot rule out that the enrichment is a generic feature of mildly beneficial-to-delete genes rather than an AMR-specific program. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] This tension is examined directly in [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--aromatic_catabolism_network--fitness_modules]], which also notes that ADP1's strongest Complex I fitness defects occur on non-aromatic, high-NADH substrates (acetate −1.55, succinate −1.39), undercutting a purely aromatic-pathway story and pointing toward broader respiratory constraints. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

A second limitation is statistical rather than biological: the **[[concepts/prevalence-ceiling]]**. Most Fitness Browser genes map to near-universal core pangenome clusters, leaving little presence/absence variance to detect co-inheritance; the Korea dataset had 95.2% of cofit pairs with undefined phi because both genes were present in all 72 genomes. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Phylogenetic confounding compounds this — cofit-pair phi was higher among closely related genomes (0.102) than medium-distance genomes (0.067), but sparse far-distance sampling prevented full separation of functional coupling from shared ancestry. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Finally, the broader concordance conflict [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--amr_cofitness_networks--annotation_gap_discovery]] warns that cross-method disagreement in this space is often attributable to annotation quality rather than biology, reinforcing that any single cofitness-derived claim should be read alongside its annotation source.
