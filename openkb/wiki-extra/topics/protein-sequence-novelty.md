# Protein Sequence Novelty

Protein sequence novelty is the degree to which a protein sequence lacks detectable evolutionary relatives and established functional or structural annotation in existing databases. The BER corpus speaks to this question unusually directly because it bridges a bacterial pangenome of over 130 million gene clusters to AlphaFold's alignment-depth statistics and to InterProScan domain calls, turning "how well is this protein known?" into a quantity that can be measured at genome scale rather than assessed case by case. That bridge — and its coverage limits — is the backbone of everything the corpus says about sequence novelty.

## What the Corpus Shows

**MSA depth as an operational proxy for embeddedness in known sequence space.** Multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) depth counts the detectable evolutionary relatives contributing to a protein's alignment. AlphaFold workflows normally treat it as a structure-prediction confidence input, but the BER analysis repurposes it as a proxy for how deeply a protein sits inside existing sequence and annotation resources [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Across 38,051,842 gene-cluster–UniProt pairs, MSA depth correlates strongly with functional annotation richness (Spearman ρ = 0.7563): mean domain hits rise from 0.59 for sequences with depth below 10 to 10.83 for sequences with depth ≥10,000, and mean distinct InterPro families rise from 0.059 to 4.601 over the same range [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. This relationship is the quantitative foundation for treating low MSA depth as a novelty signal — see [[concepts/msa-depth]].

**A pangenome gradient in representation.** Novelty is not evenly distributed across the pangenome. Core gene clusters have a median MSA depth of 15,308, versus 5,299 for auxiliary-plus-singleton clusters and 5,527 for auxiliary non-singletons, with the gap sharpest at the low tail (10th-percentile depth 334 for core versus 25–32 for accessory classes) [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Hypothetical-protein rates run in the opposite direction — 3.8% for core clusters versus 11.6% and 13.8% for the two accessory classes — showing that pangenome class (breadth of conservation) and MSA depth (density of homologous sequence evidence) are related but distinct axes of "how well do we know this gene" [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. This distinction is elaborated in [[concepts/pangenome-integration]].

**Paradox proteins: conserved yet unstudied.** The most striking novelty class is the intersection of the two axes: core clusters with MSA depth below 10, termed "paradox proteins." The analysis finds 415,603 such clusters spanning 14,768 species clades, with mean MSA depth 4.57 and median 4.0 [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Within this set, 68.9% (286,439) are hypothetical, only 0.033% (137) carry EC numbers, and 0.083% (346) map to KEGG [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Broad evolutionary conservation combined with almost no detectable homologous sequence space makes this a concrete, prioritizable hypothesis set for structural and functional follow-up, discussed in [[concepts/structural-novelty]] — though conservation alone does not establish essentiality, biochemical activity, or a genuinely novel fold [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

**Scale of coverage and the unmeasured majority.** The 38,051,842 scored pairs represent only 28.7% of the pangenome's 132,531,501 total gene clusters [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The bridging chain that produces this coverage is itself informative: of all gene clusters, 38,804,903 (29.3%) carried a real UniProt accession through `bakta_annotations.uniref100`, and 38,051,842 (28.7%) successfully bridged to AlphaFold's MSA-depth table, leaving 70.7% either lacking UniRef100 identifiers or holding only UniParc-only entries with no AlphaFold record [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Identifier mismatch is a recurring practical obstacle here: an ADP1-specific comparison found that mmseqs2-style cluster IDs and BERDL's centroid gene IDs produced 0% direct string matches, illustrating how apparent absence of data can reflect missing relational bridges rather than missing biology [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]. The BERDL Data Atlas catalogs a much larger AlphaFold reference layer (241,070,489 predicted structures) alongside 215,130,942 UniProt proteins and 475,217,233 UniRef100 clusters [src: berdl_data_atlas], but a large reference collection does not itself resolve pangenome-to-protein identifier gaps [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Because the bridged 28.7% is biased toward organisms with established reference proteomes, the true extent of novelty among the unbridged 70.7% is likely underestimated [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

**Limits of what the novelty proxies can tell us.** The corpus is explicit that MSA depth measures embeddedness in current knowledge, not causation of annotation quality, and it does not by itself demonstrate a novel fold [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. A separate, corpus-wide null result adds a caution against over-reading conservation more generally: pangenome openness (the proportion of accessory genome) shows no significant relationship with either environmental or phylogenetic effects on gene content (openness vs. environment: rho = -0.05, p = 0.54; openness vs. phylogeny: rho = 0.03, p = 0.73) [src: pangenome_openness], meaning that gross pangenome-structure metrics should not be assumed to track the ecological or evolutionary forces that produce sequence novelty.

## Tensions and Caveats

The central unresolved question is whether conservation (core-genome membership) should be read as evidence of biological importance and — by extension — as a reason to expect eventual annotation. [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_deletion_phenotypes--alphafold_msa_annotation]] lays this out directly: one line of evidence treats core status as a real signal of essentiality and representation, while another shows that many conserved, low-MSA-depth core clusters remain hypothetical regardless of their conservation [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. For sequence novelty specifically, this means "core" cannot be used as a shortcut for "well understood" — the paradox-protein set is direct proof that conservation and annotation richness can dissociate sharply within the same pangenome class.

A second caveat is architectural rather than biological: the Atlas explicitly distinguishes a schema-level bridge (tables that can technically be joined) from a biologically validated join (tables whose values actually correspond) and notes that not all cross-tenant analyses have been validated to the latter standard [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Novelty statistics computed on the AlphaFold bridge inherit this caveat — coverage is real, but its representativeness across taxa is not guaranteed.

Finally, the reported MSA-depth/domain-richness correlation (ρ = 0.7563) was not stratified by pangenome class or organism-level annotation bias, and MSA depth is assigned per representative sequence rather than across the full within-cluster diversity, so cluster-level novelty estimates may smooth over real heterogeneity [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The AlphaFold-linked database is also a static snapshot; as sequence databases grow, today's "novel" low-depth proteins may become well-represented, so novelty rankings should be treated as time-stamped rather than fixed [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].
