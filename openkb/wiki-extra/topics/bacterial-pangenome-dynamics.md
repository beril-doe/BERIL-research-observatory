# Bacterial Pangenome Dynamics

A bacterial species' pangenome — the union of all genes found across its strains — is not a static catalog but a dynamic system in which a conserved core, a fluctuating accessory compartment, and a stream of horizontally acquired or lost genes interact under ecological and mobile-element pressures. This corpus assembles genome-scale, annotation-based, and comparative-ecological evidence — spanning tens of thousands of species and MAGs — that treats the pangenome as a lens for understanding how bacteria acquire, retain, lose, and deploy genetic capacity in response to metals, phages, carbon variability, and other environmental forces. The recurring question across projects is when accessory-genome structure reflects genuine ecological adaptation and mobile-element traffic, and when it instead reflects confounds like genome size, phylogeny, or detection limits.

## What the Corpus Shows

**A two-speed genome organizes core and accessory function.** Across 357,623 genes from 32 species and 9 phyla, novel/singleton genes are strongly enriched for mobile-element functions (COG L, +10.88%, 100% consistency) and defense mechanisms (COG V, +2.83%, 100% consistency), while core genes are depleted in several central metabolic categories [src: cog_analysis]. This structural split is refined, not overturned, by finer analyses: only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families (5.0%) are universally essential across 48 organisms, and just 15 families are essential in all 48 [src: essential_genome], while ecologically important plant-growth-promoting (PGP) genes are overwhelmingly core, with mean accessory fraction 29.7% versus a 53.2% genome-wide baseline [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. Within species, gene-content ecotypes reveal that even the "fast" compartment is functionally structured: 170 of 257 species-by-COG tests were significant, with defense (V) and mobility-related (L) categories differentiated in 11/12 and 9/12 species respectively [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. See [[concepts/two-speed-genome]].

**Mobile genetic elements are the dominant engine of accessory-genome turnover.** Costly, dispensable genes (5,526 of 142,190 surveyed) are 7.45-fold enriched for mobile-element keywords relative to costly-but-conserved genes (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71), and the SEED "Phages, Prophages, Transposable elements, Plasmids" category is 11.7-fold enriched [src: costly_dispensable_genes]. This pattern generalizes environmentally: among 30,497 environmental MAGs, 21.8% carry T4SS/conjugative machinery, and 92 CAZy families — led by GT2 glycosyltransferases — co-occur with T4SS loci within 10 kb, with 32 high-confidence cross-phylum HGT events detected in a GT2 gene tree [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. Defense systems and AMR show the same accessory bias: across 27,690 species, defense-system families like Retron, Gabija, and R-M Type II are 8–12% core against a 46.8% genome-wide baseline [src: phage_defense_arsenal], and 52.9% of 27,702 species carry both AMR and prophage markers, with prophage density predicting AMR breadth (Spearman ρ=0.572, p<10⁻³⁰⁰) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. See [[concepts/mobile-genetic-elements]] and [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer]].

**Accessory traits scale up to ecological niche breadth.** Metal-resistance breadth — the diversity of metal types resisted rather than gene count — positively predicts phylogenetically corrected niche breadth across 606 genera (β=+0.021, p=1.5×10⁻⁴), a result that survives genome-size, species-count, and rarefaction controls [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. This breadth-over-depth signature recurs in carbon storage: PHB biosynthesis (phaC) is present in 21.9% of 27,690 species and tracks environmental variability, from 44.0% prevalence in plant-associated habitats to 3.3% in animal-associated ones (chi²=1,656.36) [src: phb_granule_ecology], consistent with [[concepts/feast-famine-selection]]. Both projects converge on genome size as a major confound requiring explicit correction — see [[concepts/ecological-generalism]] and [[concepts/metal-resistance-breadth]].

**Defense repertoires expand under phage pressure, layered with mobility.** Across 7,323 species, defense-system count correlates with prophage-cluster burden (ρ=0.609; partial ρ=0.301 after controlling for genome size and phylum), a pattern holding across all nine tested phyla [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Defense systems also co-occur nonrandomly (27 of 28 pairs enriched; strongest pair OR=24.0), suggesting layered repertoires rather than independent accumulation [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. The SNIPE defense system extends this picture: 4,572 clusters across 33 phyla, 86.7% accessory or singleton [src: snipe_defense_system]. See [[concepts/microbial-arms-race]].

**Openness and gene-content variation do not automatically signal adaptation.** Pangenome openness itself is a weak predictor of *why* gene content varies: openness correlates with environment effects at ρ=-0.05 (p=0.54) and phylogeny effects at ρ=0.03 (p=0.73) [src: pangenome_openness]. This caution matters because several ecological-breadth and HGT claims in this corpus rest on inferring process from accessory-genome structure alone.

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus contains real, unresolved disagreements rather than a single consensus model. First, **core status is an unreliable proxy for functional importance in either direction**: 859 families are essential wherever present, but only 15 are universal; core genes are simultaneously more likely to be essential *and* more likely to show burdensome or beneficial-to-delete phenotypes in lab assays [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis]]. Second, the same accessory, mobile-element-associated gene class is read as either decaying evolutionary debris or retained adaptive cargo — costly+dispensable genes are framed as HGT junk [src: costly_dispensable_genes], while defense and CAZi–T4SS loci with comparable mobility signatures show strong retained function [[conflicts/conflict--costly_dispensable_genes--ecotype_functional_differentiation--phage_defense_arsenal]]. Third, HGT signatures are gene-set-specific, not universal: PGP genes reject an HGT-centered model outright, with all 13 markers above the 46.8% core baseline, even as costly-dispensable, T4SS-CAZy, prophage-AMR, and phaC-acquisition evidence all point toward transfer in other gene sets [[conflicts/conflict--costly_dispensable_genes--pgp_pangenome_ecology--phb_granule_ecology]]. Fourth, whether ecological/field relevance adds conservation power beyond general fitness importance is contested, with metal-important gene conservation ranging from 71.2% to 87.4% core depending on scope [[conflicts/conflict--field_vs_lab_fitness--fitness_effects_conservation--metal_cross_resistance]]. Finally, even "dark" (unannotated) accessory genes disagree on where true novelty resides — broadly conserved and tractable, or narrow, restricted, and Methanococcus-heavy [[conflicts/conflict--functional_dark_matter--truly_dark_genes]]. Across nearly all projects, association is not causation: HGT events are inferred from discordance or co-localization, not direct reconciliation or transfer capture [src: phb_granule_ecology, t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt, prophage_amr_comobilization].
