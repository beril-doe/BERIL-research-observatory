# Sampling Bias in Metagenomics

Metagenomic studies routinely draw environmental, geographic, or ecological conclusions from collections of samples that are unevenly sampled across studies, locations, reference databases, and sequencing batches. This corpus repeatedly documents the same underlying failure mode across very different domains — soil genomics, eukaryotic contamination in prokaryote-targeted metagenomes, global metal-resistance biogeography, and warming-experiment horizon comparisons — namely that structure in *how* samples were collected can imitate or mask structure in *what biology is present*. Because several independent projects converge on this problem using different datasets and statistical designs, the corpus is unusually well-suited to characterizing sampling bias as a general methodological hazard rather than a one-off artifact of a single study.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Study identity can masquerade as environmental biology.** The clearest demonstration comes from an NMDC cross-study analysis of 2,759 ReadbasedAnalysis runs from nine studies, where sample matrix or biome categories were 80–100% nested within individual studies [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. A strong univariate association between eukaryotic fraction and matrix (Kruskal–Wallis H=77.8, p=1.3×10⁻¹⁷) collapsed once entire studies were held out: out-of-study R² fell to −0.30, and detection AUC dropped to 0.56, essentially chance [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Notably, within the single largest constituent study (NEON soil, 1,186 runs), local vegetation and geography *did* predict eukaryotic fraction (5-fold R²=+0.17 ± 0.06), showing that environmental signal can be real within a controlled batch while being an artifact across batches [[concepts/batch-confounding]]. The same logic — that apparent regional or ecological patterns may reflect where and how sampling occurred rather than biology — recurs in the global metal-resistance biogeography work, where 11 candidate geographic hotspots (e.g., Atacama/Andean Chile: 21.8% prevalence, OR=9.83, q=7.6×10⁻¹²) are explicitly flagged as pending sampling-effort and expedition-clustering correction [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] [[concepts/spatial-sampling-effort]].

**Reference and taxonomic coverage gaps distort what "signal" even means.** The ENIGMA contamination workflow retained 108 samples with 1,392 observed genera, but only 530 mapped to the GTDB-to-pangenome bridge, leaving 862 unmapped; mean mapped abundance fraction was 0.343 (range 0.031–0.854), and a stricter species-proxy mode dropped mean mapped fraction to 0.031 [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. In the NMDC eukaryotic-fraction study, Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced near-zero domain-level Eukaryota signal because their databases were prokaryote-restricted, leaving GOTTCHA2 as the only usable estimator (detectable in 77% of runs, median 2.7%, mean 13.3%, but only as a database-dependent relative estimate) [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Both cases show that an "absent" signal can reflect inadequate reference coverage rather than biological absence [[concepts/coverage-limited-inference]].

**Missing geographic coordinates create blind spots that are separable from unsampled regions.** In the global metal-resistance MAG dataset, only 22,356 of 30,497 environmental MAGs (73.3%) had usable coordinates, and an independent ENA retrieval found only 16,964 of 24,511 records (69.2%) geolocatable — a 30.8% per-sample spatial gap [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. Critically, this gap was record-level, not regional: all 532 grid cells represented in the MAG data also had ENA coordinate coverage [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] [[concepts/geospatial-coverage-gaps]]. Biome composition compounds this: soil MAGs showed 5.8% metal-resistance prevalence (OR=5.05, q=1.8×10⁻⁸²) versus 1.2% in marine MAGs (OR=0.20, q=9.2×10⁻⁸⁰), so any bias in which biomes retain coordinates could propagate into biome-level prevalence claims [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography].

**Negative out-of-sample prediction is itself a diagnostic finding, not just a null result.** In the soil-frontier genomics analysis of 5,441 soil samples, all three predictor-family models (soil-climate, geochemical, industrial) produced negative cross-validated R² (−0.205 ± 0.197, −0.331 ± 0.071, −0.221 ± 0.042), and a clay-shield hypothesis test likewise gave negative R² in both low-clay and high-clay strata with a difference CI spanning zero [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. The report treats this explicitly as ambiguous between distributional shift, outlier leverage, batch confounding, and genuine unpredictability, refusing to over-interpret negative R² as a biological null until spatial blocking and leverage diagnostics are run [src: soil_frontier_genomics] [[concepts/negative-out-of-sample-prediction]].

**Even tightly controlled experiments show that sampling context (horizon, incubation) filters the detectable signal.** The Harvard Forest warming experiment shows horizon explaining 30.6% of genus-level Bray–Curtis variation and treatment-by-horizon 41% [src: harvard_forest_warming], with warming-associated KO effects weakly correlated between organic and mineral horizons (DNA Pearson r=0.075; RNA Pearson r=0.034) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Because all organic-horizon DNA samples were lab-incubated while mineral DNA samples were direct, incubation and horizon are not fully separable — a batch-like confound embedded in the experimental design itself [src: harvard_forest_warming] [[concepts/horizon-specific-warming-response]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's central methodological tension is captured in [[conflicts/conflict--enigma_contamination_functional_potential--euk_in_prok_correlates--soil_frontier_genomics]]: coverage-adjusted or exploratory models repeatedly detect positive associations (e.g., an FDR-significant relaxed-mode defense coefficient, q=0.0462, in ENIGMA; matrix association in NMDC; frontier-area pH differences of 6.74 vs 5.94 in soil genomics), while predeclared confirmatory tests, whole-study holdouts, or effort-controlled analyses just as consistently null them out [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. The reconciliation hypotheses offered — power/scope mismatch, locality (signal is real but study-specific), and resolution-cost tradeoffs from finer mapping — are plausible but none is confirmed, meaning readers should treat any single "significant" association in this corpus as provisional until it survives a held-out-study or effort-normalized test.

A second caveat is that fixing coordinate coverage does not fix sampling-effort bias: the metal-resistance biogeography analysis shows a location can be fully coordinate-covered while still being represented by wildly different MAG counts, so geographic "hotspots" remain candidate patterns pending log(n_MAGs) normalization and expedition-clustering checks [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. Third, negative out-of-sample R² is diagnostically ambiguous by design — the soil-frontier report explicitly withholds a "biological unpredictability" interpretation until spatial shift and outlier leverage are ruled out [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. Finally, experimental confounds are not limited to observational metagenomics: the Harvard Forest incubation/horizon entanglement shows that even a controlled 25-year field experiment can carry an unresolved sampling-batch structure [src: harvard_forest_warming].
