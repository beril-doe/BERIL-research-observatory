# Gene Function Prioritization

Roughly a quarter of the genes in large bacterial fitness-screening catalogs have no confident functional annotation, and even among the deeply studied literature record, most genes and organisms are barely mentioned at all. **Gene function prioritization** is the corpus's answer to a practical question that follows from this: given finite experimental capacity, which unknown genes should be tested first, and with what method? The topic sits at the intersection of computational evidence integration, genomic context inference, evolutionary conservation, and the sociology of research attention — because a gene's apparent "darkness" can reflect either genuine biological novelty or simply an outdated annotation pipeline or a literature record that never mentioned it. This corpus treats prioritization as a triangulation problem, not a single score, and repeatedly shows that any one evidence axis used alone is misleading.

## What the Corpus Shows

**The scale and structure of the unknown.** The functional dark matter study catalogued 57,011 dark genes among 228,709 genes across 48 Fitness Browser organisms — 24.9% of the catalog — of which 17,344 had experimentally actionable evidence, split between 7,787 genes with strong fitness effects (`|fitness| ≥ 2`) and 9,557 essential genes lacking viable transposon mutants [src: functional_dark_matter]. A follow-up census decomposed this population further: 33,105 were annotation-lag genes, 6,427 were truly dark, and 17,479 were unlinked [src: truly_dark_genes]. Critically, of the 39,532 dark genes with pangenome links, 83.7% were reclassified as non-hypothetical by Bakta v1.12.0 [src: truly_dark_genes] — see [[entities/bakta]] — leaving only 16.3% truly dark in the assessed population. This single number reframes the whole topic: most "unknown" genes are an [[concepts/annotation-gap]] artifact, not evidence of novel biology, a distinction elaborated in [[concepts/experimental-functional-prioritization]].

**Two prioritization routes, two different objectives.** The corpus documents an evidence-weighted route that scores genes on six dimensions (fitness importance 0.25, conservation 0.20, inference quality 0.20, pangenome distribution 0.15, biogeographic evidence 0.10, tractability 0.10), ranking 17,344 actionable genes and surfacing a top-100 list spanning 22 organisms, 82 of which had ≥3 converging evidence types [src: functional_dark_matter]. The leading candidate, *Pseudomonas putida* N2C3 AO356_11255, combined a D-alanyl-D-alanine carboxypeptidase prediction, an EamA domain, a nitrogen fitness effect of 3.4, and a carrier-environment odds ratio of 44 [src: functional_dark_matter]. A separate, complementary route targets the truly dark residue: a multi-criteria score over 6,427 truly dark genes produced 100 candidates (scores 8–10, 19 organisms), including 34 essential genes, 53 in operons, and 30 in ICA fitness modules [src: truly_dark_genes]. These routes are not redundant — see [[concepts/experimental-functional-prioritization]] and the [[conflicts/conflict--functional_dark_matter--truly_dark_genes]] tension below.

**Genomic context as a force-multiplier, with a calibration caveat.** [[concepts/gene-neighborhood-inference]] shows how proximity, synteny, and co-fitness convert an otherwise silent gene into a testable hypothesis. Using an operon-like heuristic (same strand, <300bp gap, five-gene window), 52.9% of dark genes shared a predicted operon with an annotated gene [src: functional_dark_matter]. But the headline statistic that 97.2% of dark genes have *some* annotated neighbor is a weak signal on its own, since the background genome is ~75% annotated; the more diagnostic figure is the depressed mean annotated-neighbor fraction of 63.6%, suggesting dark genes cluster together in uncharacterized regions [src: functional_dark_matter]. Layering conserved synteny (17,058 of 21,011 orthologous pairs conserved in ≥1 other genome, median conservation score 0.95) and co-fitness (2,899 of 32,075 pairs with rank-20 co-fitness) yields 998 pairs with both signals — the corpus's strongest neighborhood-based leads [src: functional_dark_matter]. This graded confidence scheme is essential for essential genes, which lack transposon fitness data altogether and require [[entities/random-barcode-transposon-sequencing]]-independent alternatives like CRISPRi.

**Conservation is a real but weak proxy for importance.** [[concepts/fitness-conservation]] shows that fitness magnitude correlates with pangenome conservation across ~194,000 genes in 43 organisms, but modestly [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. Essential genes are 82% core versus 66.4% for genes with only mild fitness effects [src: fitness_effects_conservation], and ICA fitness modules are enriched for core genes (86.0% vs 81.5% baseline) [src: module_conservation]. But condition type is a weak discriminator: field-specific genes are not more conserved than lab-specific genes in *D. vulgaris* [src: field_vs_lab_fitness], and metal-stress genes show conservation ranging from 71.2% (condition-specific heavy-metal set) to 87.4% (pan-bacterial atlas) to 84.8–88.0% (metal-specific vs. general-sick genes) depending entirely on which population is measured [src: field_vs_lab_fitness, metal_fitness_atlas, metal_specificity]. Conservation should inform prioritization scores, but it cannot substitute for direct phenotypic evidence.

**Literature attention is not evidence of function, and its absence is not evidence of novelty.** [[concepts/literature-coverage-bias]] and [[concepts/research-attention-inequality]] quantify why "unstudied" gene lists must be interpreted cautiously. In PaperBLAST, *Homo sapiens* alone accounts for 46.7% of gene-paper records, the organism-level Gini coefficient is 0.967, and 65.6% of the 841K linked genes have exactly one paper [src: paperblast_explorer]. At the protein-family level, 55.3% of 50%-identity families have zero or one paper, and 5,218 multi-member families (14,534 sequences) have no literature coverage at all [src: paperblast_explorer]. Because this reflects indexed PubMed Central text-mining rather than total biological knowledge, a "dark" designation from any resource — Fitness Browser, PaperBLAST, or otherwise — is a candidate flag for missing knowledge, not proof of it [src: paperblast_explorer]. The 129,823 VIMSS cross-references linking PaperBLAST to the Fitness Browser are flagged as an unexploited opportunity to test whether fitness-important genes are disproportionately understudied [src: paperblast_explorer].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's two flagship dark-gene prioritization efforts disagree about where genuine darkness lives: the evidence-weighted framework favors broadly conserved, tractable organisms, while the truly-dark-genes census finds real novelty concentrated in short, accessory, taxonomically restricted genes — 55% of the truly dark set sits in Methanococcus strains alone [src: truly_dark_genes]. Both frameworks share the same 83.7% Bakta reclassification rate, but interpret it differently: one treats it as a caveat on the starting label, the other as central evidence that "dark" is mostly an annotation-currency artifact [src: functional_dark_matter, truly_dark_genes]. See [[conflicts/conflict--functional_dark_matter--truly_dark_genes]] for the full argument and proposed reconciliations (definitional-scope, selection-bias, pipeline-order hypotheses).

A second, broader tension concerns whether conservation is a trustworthy shortcut for importance at all. [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis]] documents a two-way mismatch: only 15 gene families are essential across all 48 sampled organisms, versus 859 essential-when-present, and core genes are simultaneously more conserved and more likely to be burdensome or condition-specific [src: essential_genome, conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Relatedly, [[conflicts/conflict--field_vs_lab_fitness--fitness_effects_conservation--metal_cross_resistance]] shows that ecological/field relevance sometimes tracks conservation and sometimes adds nothing beyond generic fitness importance, with metal-gene conservation estimates spanning 71.2%–88.0% depending on scope [src: field_vs_lab_fitness, metal_fitness_atlas, metal_specificity] — a reminder that prioritization scores built on conservation weights must specify their reference population explicitly.

Finally, neighborhood-inference confidence is easy to overstate: 97.2% "having a neighbor" is nearly guaranteed by a 75%-annotated genome background and should never be quoted as standalone support [src: functional_dark_matter].
