# Metabolic Capability Evolution

Metabolic capability evolution asks how the biosynthetic and catabolic repertoires encoded in genomes change under selection — which pathways are gained, lost, held in reserve, or diversified as lineages adapt to environments, hosts, and community contexts. This corpus approaches the question from an unusually wide angle: pangenome-scale comparative genomics across *Pseudomonas* and other taxa, community metagenome/metabolome pairing from NMDC, high-throughput fitness assays across hundreds of genome-by-condition combinations, and organism-level pathway-dependency classification. Because every one of these approaches converges on the same core distinction — that a pathway's genomic *presence* is not the same as its ecological or physiological *importance* — the topic hub exists to show how consistently that distinction reshapes claims about evolution, loss, and adaptation across otherwise independent projects.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Genomic capability and functional dependency are only loosely coupled.** The clearest organizing result is that a pathway being complete says little on its own about whether it matters. Across 1,695 pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, only 51.9% were "active dependencies," while 15.8% were classified as latent — complete but fitness-neutral under tested conditions — and 32.3% fell in between [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. This tripartite split, elaborated in [[concepts/latent-metabolic-capabilities]], varied strongly by pathway category: carbon utilization pathways were latent 24.3% of the time versus only 6.5% for amino acid biosynthesis (χ²=163.6, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The same logic appears independently in [[concepts/capability-versus-kinetics]], where genome content (KO presence/absence) predicted binary growth capability reasonably well for amino acids (AUC 0.775) and nucleosides (AUC 0.780) but failed entirely to predict continuous kinetic traits like growth rate, lag, or yield (negative cross-genus R²) [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. Evolution acts on capability, but capability and performance are evolutionarily and mechanistically distinct axes.

**Environmental supply shapes which costly functions are retained or lost.** [[concepts/black-queen-dynamics]] links this dependency structure to an evolutionary mechanism: costly biosynthetic pathways are hypothesized to be lost when the environment reliably supplies the corresponding metabolite. In NMDC soil/sediment communities, 11 of 13 testable amino acid pathways showed the predicted negative correlation between community pathway completeness and ambient metabolite intensity (binomial p = 0.011), with leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022) and arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049) surviving FDR correction — both energetically expensive pathways (37 and 26 ATP equivalents) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Organism-level data reinforces the conditional nature of this loss logic: all 66 organism–pathway pairs classified as "Latent Capability" became fitness-important under at least one tested condition (nitrogen limitation, stress, carbon limitation), meaning apparent dispensability in one medium does not imply true evolutionary neutrality [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

**Lineage-level streamlining is real but entangled with phylogeny.** [[concepts/metabolic-niche-partitioning]] and [[concepts/metabolic-streamlining]] provide the corpus's sharpest comparative-genomic evidence for adaptive pathway loss. Across 12,732 *Pseudomonas* genomes and 62 GapMind carbon pathways, the *P. aeruginosa* (sensu stricto) group lost plant-associated sugar pathways almost completely relative to *Pseudomonas_E* — xylose 0.0% vs 74.4%, arabinose 0.0% vs 62.6%, myo-inositol 0.0% vs 58.8% — while core amino-acid and organic-acid pathways stayed >99% complete in both groups [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. This selective, substrate-specific pattern is stronger evidence for adaptive streamlining than an undifferentiated loss of pathway richness would be. Yet the same analysis shows that PCA of pathway content is dominated by the deep subgenus split rather than lifestyle category, and within *Pseudomonas_E* lifestyle differences overlap substantially (balanced classifier accuracy only 0.408 ± 0.169 vs 0.250 chance) [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] — so ecological signal and phylogenetic inheritance are difficult to fully separate.

**Within-species variation ("ecotypes") shows capability evolution is ongoing, not just historical.** [[concepts/metabolic-ecotypes]] documents pathway-based subclustering within all 10 analyzed species (silhouette >0.2 in each), most strikingly six clusters within 11,396 *Salmonella enterica* genomes (silhouette 0.894) significantly associated with isolation environment (χ²=1570.2, p<0.0001) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The most heterogeneous pathways were valine/leucine (0.60), tryptophan (0.51), and lysine/threonine biosynthesis (0.49) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] — again amino-acid biosynthesis, echoing the Black Queen signal seen at the community level. Notably, marine organisms (*Pelagibacter*, *Prochlorococcus*, *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*) showed no significant environment–cluster association, suggesting either coarse metadata or that pathway-level resolution misses the relevant gene-content differences [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

**Metabolic output is itself an evolving, measurable phenotype distinct from pathway presence.** [[concepts/metabolic-novelty]] adds a production-side view: among ENIGMA isolates in Web of Microbes, de novo ("emergent") metabolite production ranged from 15.2% to 32.4% of observed changes across five strains [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]. Because growth-predictive KOs and metabolite-associated KOs barely overlap (Spearman rho = 0.043) [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma], capability, output, and kinetics appear to be three loosely coupled targets of selection rather than one unified "metabolic fitness" trait.

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's central unresolved tension is captured directly in [[conflicts/conflict--essential_metabolome--lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]]: does high prevalence of pathway-diagnostic genes establish functional completeness, or merely suggest it? The essential-metabolome analysis reports 17 of 18 amino acid pathways universal across seven organisms [src: essential_metabolome], while the lanthanide atlas shows xoxF outnumbering mxaF 18.92:1 across 293,059 genomes [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] — both framed as evidence of conservation. But 897 xoxF-bearing genomes (24.3%) had no PQQ evidence from either annotation source, and eggNOG vs Bakta lanM calls diverged by nearly an order of magnitude (505 vs 62 positives) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas], showing that "completeness" conclusions are sensitive to annotation source and definition (marker-level vs pathway-level). This matters directly for capability-evolution claims: apparent conservation or loss could reflect annotation artifacts rather than biology.

A second tension is scale-dependence of ecological signal. The *Pseudomonas* streamlining story is strong at the subgenus level (p = 0.006 for environment association) but weak within *Pseudomonas_E* (overlapping lifestyle categories, modest classifier accuracy) [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology], meaning the same dataset supports both "environment shapes metabolic capability" and "phylogeny dominates, ecology is secondary" readings depending on the resolution examined.

A third caveat is threshold and data-coverage sensitivity: the latent-capability fraction of 15.8% ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 alternative thresholds [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], and the NMDC Black Queen test excluded all 33 freshwater samples for lacking paired metabolomics, making the analysis effectively soil-only and dominated (95%) by a single study [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Finally, none of these analyses measure flux, expression, or direct metabolite exchange — every claim about "dependency," "streamlining," or "latency" is an inference from genomic potential and fitness proxies, not direct biochemical demonstration [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, pseudomonas_carbon_ecology].
