# Prophage Genome Ecology

Prophages — bacteriophage genomes integrated into bacterial chromosomes — are neither fully "phage" nor fully "host": they carry genes for their own replication and lysis alongside accessory cargo that can reshape host defense, competition, and niche range. This corpus speaks directly to prophage ecology through a single large-scale analysis that combines pangenome-wide gene-cluster detection across tens of thousands of bacterial species with independent metagenomic validation, asking a structural question: does environmental selection act on whole prophage lineages, or on the functional modules that prophages are built from? The answer reshapes how mobile genetic elements should be modeled — as inherited wholes or as recombining parts — and it connects to broader corpus themes of annotation confidence and the adaptive-versus-debris status of accessory genomic cargo.

## What the Corpus Shows

**A two-tier genome architecture separates a conserved backbone from variable accessory modules.** Across 27,702 bacterial species, seven prophage-associated functional modules were scored, and three — packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation — were essentially universal (100.0%, 99.9%, and 100.0% of species) and showed strong physical linkage: in tests across 15 phylogenetically stratified species, lysis genes co-occurred significantly in 13/15 species (mean contig co-localization 0.901–1.000 across the three core modules), consistent with a tightly linked prophage backbone [src: prophage_ecology]. By contrast, head morphogenesis, tail, and anti-defense modules occurred in only 56.1%, 55.6%, and 64.3% of species respectively, with anti-defense genes showing weak mutual co-localization (significant in only 3/11 tests, mean co-localization 0.281) — a pattern consistent with anti-defense genes residing in separable "defense islands" rather than the core prophage body [src: prophage_ecology]. This backbone/accessory split is elaborated in [[concepts/prophage-genome-modularity]].

**Environmental signal concentrates in modules, not in whole terminase lineages.** Clustering 38,085 terminase large-subunit (TerL) sequences at 70% identity produced 10,991 lineages, 63% of them singletons, yet none of these lineages showed significant environment-specific enrichment after FDR correction across 500 tests [src: prophage_ecology]. Meanwhile, constrained permutation testing (preserving host family and genome-size quartile) found eight significant module–environment associations at FDR<0.05, including enrichment of tail, head-morphogenesis, and anti-defense modules in human-associated environments (log2 odds ratios of 2.21, 1.98, and 1.70) [src: prophage_ecology]. This module-versus-lineage split is the corpus's central structural claim about prophage ecology, detailed in [[concepts/module-versus-lineage-ecology]].

**Independent metagenomic data corroborate the module-level signal.** Analysis of 6,365 NMDC metagenomic samples found 57 significant module–abiotic-variable correlations (FDR<0.05), with packaging correlating with pH (rho=0.519) and overall prophage burden correlating with pH, temperature, and depth (rho=0.474, 0.399, and 0.361 respectively) [src: prophage_ecology]. Head morphogenesis, tail, and anti-defense were significant in both the pangenome-enrichment and NMDC analyses, while the near-universal backbone modules showed little pangenome enrichment despite occasional NMDC significance — attributed to a prevalence ceiling that limits presence/absence variation once a module is nearly fixed [src: prophage_ecology]. Environmental effects were not simply genome-size or phylogeny artifacts: PERMANOVA gave F=30.04 for environment and F=6.17 for phylogeny (both p=0.01), and environmental effects held within every genome-size quartile (Kruskal-Wallis p<6.5e-78) [src: prophage_ecology].

**Lineage-level ecological breadth still varies, even without formal enrichment.** Among 824 TerL lineages with at least five species, 325 were classified as ecological specialists (Shannon entropy<1.0 or a dominant environment >80%) concentrated in animal-associated, freshwater, and marine environments, while 499 were generalists spanning at least three environments [src: prophage_ecology]. This shows that lineage breadth and statistically detected lineage enrichment are distinct measurements — a lineage can look ecologically concentrated without exceeding what host phylogeny alone predicts [src: prophage_ecology].

**Prophage-associated genes sit inside the corpus's broader annotation-gap problem.** Module assignments in the prophage study were built from eggNOG annotations rather than dedicated prophage-detection tools, meaning some "accessory" or "anti-defense" calls may include bacterial homologs or domesticated prophage remnants rather than functional prophage genes — a direct instance of the [[concepts/annotation-gap]] pattern in which annotation status depends heavily on method and database choice rather than ground truth [src: prophage_ecology, annotation_gap_discovery]. NMDC environmental inference also relied on indirect genus-level taxonomic classification, compounding this uncertainty [src: prophage_ecology].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's own internal tension is the module/lineage split itself: module composition shows strong, corrected environmental signal while no single TerL lineage does, and this should not be flattened into either "prophages are environmentally adapted" or "prophages are purely host-constrained" — it is a scale-dependent result, not a contradiction, since a lineage can be ecologically specialist in composition while failing a stricter enrichment test built on a constrained null [src: prophage_ecology].

A second, unresolved tension concerns whether accessory and defense-associated cargo (anti-defense modules, prophage-linked AMR genes) represents adaptively retained function or decaying evolutionary debris en route to loss. [[conflicts/conflict--costly_dispensable_genes--ecotype_functional_differentiation--phage_defense_arsenal]] lays out both sides: weak fine-scale AMR–prophage co-localization (only 10.4% of AMR instances within 10 genes of a marker) argues for debris, while strong defense-system co-occurrence and a positive defense-count/prophage-burden association argue for retained investment under phage pressure. The prophage_ecology data itself is consistent with either reading, since anti-defense modules are both weakly self-co-localized (mean 0.281) and strongly environment-responsive.

A related caveat concerns how much weight to put on near-universal module prevalence as evidence of biological importance. [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_deletion_phenotypes--alphafold_msa_annotation]] documents corpus-wide disagreement about whether pangenome conservation predicts function or fitness; applied here, it cautions against reading the 100%-prevalent packaging/lysis/lysogeny modules as automatically "more important" than variable accessory modules — their universality may partly reflect a prevalence ceiling that mechanically suppresses detectable environmental enrichment rather than biological indispensability alone [src: prophage_ecology].

Finally, the causal interpretation throughout is bounded: constrained permutation controls for host family and genome-size quartile, but the report does not demonstrate that environmental conditions directly select for module gain or loss, only that composition correlates with environment beyond these controls [src: prophage_ecology].
