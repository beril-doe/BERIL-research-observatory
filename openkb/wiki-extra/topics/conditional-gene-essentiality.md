# Conditional Gene Essentiality

Gene essentiality is often treated as a fixed genomic label — a gene either is or is not required for life. This corpus argues instead that essentiality is a graded, environment-dependent measurement outcome, one that shifts with carbon source, stress, antibiotic exposure, and even the assay used to probe it. The topic sits at the intersection of transposon-fitness genomics (RB-TnSeq), genome-scale metabolic modeling (FBA), and comparative pangenomics, and the BERIL corpus is unusually well positioned to speak to it because it combines a deep single-organism dataset (*Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, probed by knockout, FBA, RB-TnSeq, growth assays, and proteomics in parallel) with pan-bacterial surveys spanning tens of thousands of genes and dozens of organisms. Together these sources let us ask not just "is this gene essential?" but "essential by which method, under which condition, and does that essentiality predict anything about the gene's evolutionary fate?"

## What the Corpus Shows

**Essentiality is graded, not binary, and depends on which method asks the question.** The ADP1 triple-essentiality work distinguishes at least six non-equivalent forms of "importance" — lethality on knockout, metabolic necessity under FBA, fitness cost under RB-TnSeq, condition-specific growth optimization, expression requirement, and evolutionary conservation [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. These endpoints agree only partially: FBA-predicted essentiality reaches Cohen's κ = 0.486 in rich medium and κ = 0.493 in minimal medium against knockout lethality [src: adp1_triple_essentiality], but among 478 TnSeq-dispensable genes, FBA class shows no association with growth-defect status (chi-squared = 0.93, p = 0.63) [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. The [[concepts/method-concordance]] page frames this as a measurement-integration problem rather than a contradiction: different assays measure different biological phenomena, and disagreement at one boundary (lethality) does not imply disagreement at another (quantitative fitness cost).

**Pathway-level and genome-scale evidence confirm that "latent" capacity is really conditional necessity.** The pathway capability–dependency analysis classified 161 organism–pathway pairs across seven model bacteria; 41.0% were "Latent Capabilities" — complete pathways not important under aggregate lab conditions — yet all 66 of these pairs became fitness-important under at least one tested condition, especially nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. See [[concepts/condition-dependent-essentiality]] for the full categorical breakdown (Active Dependency, Latent Capability, Incomplete but Important, Missing).

**In ADP1, the phenotype landscape is mostly a continuum with one sharp exception.** Hierarchical clustering of 2,034 genes across eight carbon sources yields a weak optimal clustering (K = 3, silhouette = 0.24), consistent with graded, overlapping sensitivity rather than discrete functional modules [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. The one clear exception is a 24–51 gene quinate-specific module (mean quinate z-score −7.28) tied to aromatic catabolism [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. The [[concepts/phenotypic-landscape]] page traces how this module resolves into distributed support functions — the aromatic pathway itself, PQQ biosynthesis, iron acquisition, and Complex I — via [[concepts/metabolic-support-networks]] [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].

**Conditional essentiality can arise from respiratory bottlenecks that FBA cannot see.** Complex I comprises 21 of 51 quinate-specific genes (41%) and receives 1.76× higher predicted flux on aromatic substrates (0.55 vs 0.31), yet FBA assigns it 0% essentiality [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]. The [[concepts/nadh-flux-respiratory-constraints]] page argues this reflects NADH production *rate*, not total yield: quinate yields only 4 theoretical NADH molecules versus 9 for glucose, yet Complex I is essential on quinate and dispensable on glucose, consistent with a concentrated TCA-cycle burst overwhelming lower-capacity alternative dehydrogenases [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]. Linear-programming models miss this because they can redistribute flux around a linear constraint, but cannot represent the threshold failure of a multi-subunit complex [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].

**Conditional essentiality does not translate cleanly into conservation, but it correlates.** Essential genes are 82% core, while genes neutral in every experiment are still 66% core — a real but modest gradient [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Genes with strong condition-specific effects are 1.78× more likely to be core, and genes with a true trade-off profile (important in some conditions, burdensome in others; 25,271 genes, 17.8% of the analyzed set) are 1.29× more likely to be core (OR = 1.29, p = 1.2e-44) [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs]. This burden effect is function-specific: core genes show excess burden in Protein Metabolism (+6.2pp), Motility (+7.8pp), and RNA Metabolism (+12.9pp), but the reverse pattern in Cell Wall (−14.1pp) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. See [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] and [[concepts/core-gene-tradeoffs|core_gene_tradeoffs]] for the full evolutionary framing.

**Gapfilled model structure and environmental assumptions compound conditionality.** Because 87% of ADP1 growth-phenotype predictions (105,376 of 121,519) depend on at least one gapfilled reaction [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer], apparent condition-dependence in FBA output can reflect gapfill artifacts as much as biology. Aromatic degradation genes are strongly enriched among FBA-discordant genes (9 of 11, OR = 9.70, q = 0.012), with a directional bias toward FBA under-prediction [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] — a pattern the [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] page interprets as consistent with a mismatch between the model's minimal-media definition and possible trace aromatic compounds in the real experimental medium, though this remains an untested hypothesis [src: adp1_triple_essentiality].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus does not converge on a single account of how much of the genome is condition-dependent, or how conservation and importance relate. Two ADP1 analyses disagree sharply on the *scope* of condition-dependence: the full deletion matrix finds 625 of 2,034 genes (31%) reach a condition-specificity threshold, while the triple-essentiality subset finds 333 of 478 genes (70%) show at least one condition-specific defect [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes, adp1_triple_essentiality]. The gap may reflect gene-set coverage (the subset may already be enriched for near-essential genes) or differing statistical thresholds rather than a real biological disagreement — see [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_deletion_phenotypes--adp1_triple_essentiality--amr_cofitness_networks]].

A second, deeper tension concerns whether FBA is a validated predictor of essentiality at all. Moderate genome-wide concordance (κ ≈ 0.49) coexists with a null result for growth-defect magnitude among dispensable genes (p = 0.63) and with the Complex I flux/essentiality mismatch described above [src: adp1_triple_essentiality, aromatic_catabolism_network]. The [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery]] page proposes that FBA may be a valid *threshold* (lethal/non-lethal) model without being a valid *continuous* cost model — a distinction the corpus has not yet tested directly.

Third, the relationship between conservation and fitness importance is genuinely two-directional and only partly reconciled. Only 15 ortholog families were essential in all 48 sampled organisms, versus 859 essential in every organism where the family was present at all [src: essential_genome] — a gap that may be purely definitional (conditioning on presence vs. universal presence) [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Meanwhile, 24.4% of core genes show *positive* fitness when deleted, versus 19.9% of accessory genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis], undercutting any simple "core = beneficial to keep" heuristic. See [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis]].

Finally, cofitness-based inference of shared function — heavily used to resolve which genes belong to a conditional support network — cannot cleanly distinguish mechanistic coupling from convergent, independent dispensability under the same condition [src: amr_cofitness_networks, aromatic_catabolism_network]; see [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--aromatic_catabolism_network--fitness_modules]].
