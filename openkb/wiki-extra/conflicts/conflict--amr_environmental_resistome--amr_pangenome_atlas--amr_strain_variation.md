# Clinical AMR Enrichment: Ecological Signal or Sampling Artifact?

Across the [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] work, several independent findings converge on a single unresolved question: is the strong association between clinical isolation and AMR gene abundance a real biological signal — driven by antibiotic selection, niche breadth, and lineage-restricted acquisition — or is it substantially an artifact of database composition, incomplete metadata, and annotation scope? The same tension recurs at the level of core/accessory classification, mechanism labeling, and temporal trend detection, making it a cross-cutting methodological dispute rather than an isolated disagreement.

## Evidence Sides

**For a real ecological/causal signal**
Core AMR genes are treated as a proxy for intrinsic resistance and accessory AMR genes as a proxy for acquired resistance, consistent with prior resistome observations. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_pangenome_atlas] A positive association between environmental diversity and AMR gene count is read as support for a niche-breadth/horizontal-acquisition hypothesis. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Non-core AMR genes show stronger phylogenetic signal than core genes, suggesting lineage-restricted maintenance rather than random distribution. [src: amr_strain_variation]

**For sampling bias and annotation artifact**
Clinical isolates are massively overrepresented in NCBI and more likely to be sequenced because of their resistance phenotypes, while environmental AMR may be underestimated when novel resistance genes are missed by annotations focused on known determinants. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Only 7,838 of 14,723 AMR-carrying species (53.2%) received a non-Other/Unknown environment classification, leaving 46.8% unclassified. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Separately, 52.7% of genomes lacked a classifiable isolation source for ecotype testing. [src: amr_strain_variation] The atlas's 83,008 AMRFinderPlus hits across 1,939 gene families include non-antibiotic stress-response genes, and mechanism labels generated by keyword matching leave 22.2% Other/Unclassified. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Visible environment-associated ecotypes are not supported by adequately powered statistical tests for most species. [src: amr_strain_variation] No significant temporal trend in AMR gene count survived multiple-testing correction across 513 species (≥20 genomes, ≥3 post-1990 years), with roughly balanced slopes (251 positive vs. 262 negative) and only 70% of genomes carrying parseable dates. [src: amr_strain_variation]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Measurement-difference hypothesis**: both effects may be real but confounded — genuine antibiotic selection in clinical settings coexists with reverse-causality sampling (resistant organisms preferentially sequenced), and current designs cannot separate them. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- **Scope-difference hypothesis**: core/accessory status may correctly track *some* intrinsic/acquired resistance while failing for mobile genes fixed in a lineage, since prevalence alone doesn't establish mobility or acquisition history. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_strain_variation]
- **Definitional hypothesis**: the temporal null result may reflect a data-quality ceiling (sparse dates, mechanism ontology) rather than genuine absence of environmental selection pressure over time.

## Resolving Work

- Rarefaction or resampling analyses that equalize clinical vs. environmental sequencing depth to test whether enrichment persists.
- Independent, function-based annotation (e.g., HMM/structure-based) to catch AMR genes missed by AMRFinderPlus keyword matching.
- Curated, complete isolation-source and collection-date metadata for the unclassified 46.8%/52.7% fractions to re-run ecotype and temporal tests with full power.
- Phylogenetically controlled causal-inference designs (e.g., ancestral state reconstruction) to distinguish selection from reverse-causal sampling.
- Mechanism reclassification using a shared, non-keyword ontology to test whether the 22.2% Other/Unclassified genes shift core/accessory or clinical/environmental comparisons.
