# Pangenome Integration

Pangenome integration connects gene, cluster, and functional-annotation records generated by different analyses despite incompatible identifiers and clustering schemes. In the BERDL corpus, it provides comparative context for gene essentiality, condition-specific fitness, deletion-collection coverage, structural novelty, environmental resistomes, AMR strain variation, resistance-associated fitness, metabolic annotation gaps, environmental metal tolerance, community metabolism, and cross-tenant data discovery. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, adp1_deletion_phenotypes, alphafold_msa_annotation, amr_environmental_resistome, amr_fitness_cost, amr_strain_variation, annotation_gap_discovery, bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance, berdl_data_atlas, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The integration layer is not merely an identifier-matching utility. It joins pangenome conservation and gene presence with experimental phenotypes, phylogeny, environmental metadata, structural representation, resistance mechanisms, metabolic reactions, sequence homology, isolation ecology, genome-based metal-tolerance scores, and community metabolomics. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, alphafold_msa_annotation, amr_environmental_resistome, amr_strain_variation, annotation_gap_discovery, bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The BERDL Data Atlas extends this view from individual analyses to the lakehouse as a whole: it identified 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases, 17 tenants, 10 funding agencies or programs, and 17 biological topics, exposing 29 canonical join keys and 536 schema-level cross-tenant bridges. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The NMDC–pangenome analysis demonstrated that this integration surface can support community-level ecological inference: 220 NMDC communities were bridged to GTDB pangenome species with mean coverage of 94.6%, and GapMind pathway completeness was compared with metabolite intensities and ecosystem type. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

A complementary pangenome-openness analysis found no significant relationship between openness and either environmental or phylogenetic effects on gene content. Openness versus environment had Spearman rho = -0.05 and p-value = 0.54, while openness versus phylogeny had rho = 0.03 and p-value = 0.73. [src: pangenome_openness] Thus, an open or closed pangenome should not be treated as a proxy for whether ecology or ancestry dominates gene-content variation. This null result suggests that openness is a coarse descriptor of variability, that horizontal gene transfer may be opportunistic rather than niche-directed, or that core/accessory classes do not isolate the genes responsible for functional adaptation; these interpretations remain hypotheses rather than direct mechanistic findings. [src: pangenome_openness]

These connections enable questions about whether biological patterns reflect gene inheritance, ecology, sampling, model uncertainty, database coverage, identifier design, or community metabolic potential. They also require a distinction between a schema-level bridge and a biologically valid join: the Atlas validated value-space overlap for its FitnessBrowser–AlphaFold use case, but not for all proposed cross-tenant analyses. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

## Core problem

The ADP1 database uses mmseqs2-style cluster identifiers such as `NHSXFYEX_mmseqsCluster_NNNN`, whereas BERDL uses centroid gene identifiers such as `NC_005966.1_1024`; direct string matching produced 0% matches. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] This illustrates that apparent biological absence can instead result from missing relational metadata connecting independently generated representations. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

A related issue arises when pangenome clusters are connected to AlphaFold records. Of 132,531,501 gene clusters, 38,804,903 (29.3%) had a real UniProt accession through `bakta_annotations.uniref100`, and 38,051,842 (28.7%) bridged successfully to `kescience_alphafold.alphafold_msa_depths`; 70.7% lacked UniRef100 identifiers or had UniParc-only identifiers without AlphaFold entries. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] The BERDL Atlas identifies a much larger AlphaFold reference layer of 241,070,489 predicted structures, but a large reference collection does not itself resolve missing or incompatible pangenome-to-protein identifiers. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

The Atlas catalogs 1,011,650,903 KBase pangenome gene rows across 293,059 genomes, 27,690 GTDB species clades or pangenomes, and 132.5 million gene clusters. It also inventories 215,130,942 UniProt proteins, 475,217,233 UniRef100 clusters, 188,848,220 UniRef90 clusters, 60,315,044 UniRef50 clusters, approximately 253,000 experimental PDB structures, and 27,410,721 FitnessBrowser measurements. These counts establish integration potential rather than unique biological entities because the Atlas generally reports table-row counts and does not deduplicate records across tenants. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

The environmental resistome analysis required joins among AMR gene clusters, pangenome species, genome-level environmental metadata, species-level majority-vote environments, taxonomy, and continuous environmental embeddings. The resulting dataset linked 82,908 AMR gene clusters across 14,723 species with 280,337 genomes carrying environmental metadata. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The strain-variation analysis used a related integration across 1,305 species and 180,025 genomes, connecting AMR presence/absence matrices to species-level prevalence, resistance-island structure, ANI distances, ecotype assignments, collection dates, and environment labels. [src: amr_strain_variation]

The BacDive validation adds isolation-environment metadata as another integration layer. Exact species-name matching plus GTDB suffix removal linked 42,227 of 97,334 BacDive strains (43.4%) to metal scores across 6,426 GTDB species; 25,089 linked strains had isolation-source metadata. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] A phenotype-focused bridge used a related procedure: 37,368 of 97,334 strains (38.4%) matched a pangenome species and metal score, representing 5,647 unique GTDB species. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The two bridges use different matching and filtering procedures, so their coverage figures are not interchangeable. [src: bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The AMR fitness-cost analysis joined pangenome AMR annotations to Fitness Browser genes, gene-level fitness matrices, experiment classifications, and conservation labels. It identified 1,352 AMR genes across 43 organisms, linked 28 organisms to fitness matrices, and retained 25 organisms for tests requiring at least five AMR genes. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Metabolic annotation-gap discovery adds a reaction-centered integration problem. Across 14 Fitness Browser organisms and 18 carbon sources, 201 enzymatic reaction–organism pairs generated by conditional ModelSEED gapfilling were compared with pangenome gene clusters, Fitness Browser fitness evidence, GapMind pathway predictions, Bakta annotations, and Swiss-Prot homology. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The master table retained evidence and confidence at the reaction–organism level rather than collapsing every observation directly to species. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

The NMDC community-metabolic analysis combined Centrifuge profiles, omics-file and study bridges, metabolomics, and abiotic-feature tables with GapMind pathway scores, GTDB species-clade mappings, and pangenome statistics. The community pathway matrix contained 220 samples and 80 pathways; 175 samples had metabolomics overlap and 131 had both amino-acid pathway completeness and metabolomics data. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] All 220 samples passed the 30% taxonomy-bridge QC threshold, and 92% mapped at least 85% of community abundance to GTDB pangenome species. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

This community bridge supports a distinction between genomic potential and ambient chemistry. Across 13 testable amino-acid pathways, 11 (85%) showed negative correlations between community pathway completeness and metabolite intensity; leucine and arginine remained significant after FDR correction. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The result is consistent with a weak community-scale [[concepts/black-queen-dynamics]] signal, but it does not establish expression, flux, or causal metabolite exchange because GapMind measures pathway potential rather than activity. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The phenotype-tolerance analysis adds a further distinction between broad physiological traits and molecular predictors. Ten BacDive features were tested against genome-based metal-tolerance scores: eight binary features and two continuous breadth measures. Seven features passed FDR correction, but phenotype features did not improve a taxonomy-based model; adding `n_metal_clusters` raised the full-model R² to 0.633. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

These examples show that integration must preserve several levels of representation: gene, cluster, reaction, genome, strain, species, lineage, environment, isolation source, condition, experiment, community, pathway, and metabolite. Collapsing all observations directly to species can simplify analysis but may hide within-species structure, as shown by extensive strain-level AMR variation and limited power for some species-level environmental tests. [src: amr_strain_variation, amr_environmental_resistome] Conversely, aggregating reaction or pathway evidence across organisms can make conserved metabolic functions detectable while transferring annotations across phylogenetic boundaries where sequence or pathway context is not comparable. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

## ADP1–BERDL integration bridge

The ADP1 analysis resolved the identifier mismatch through BERDL's `gene_genecluster_junction` table. BERDL cluster IDs were linked to member gene IDs, those genes were matched to the `feature_id` column in ADP1's `pan_genome_features` table, and the ADP1 records supplied the corresponding ADP1-style `cluster_id`. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

This indirect bridge mapped all 4,891 BERDL clusters to 4,081 unique ADP1 clusters and achieved 100% gene-level matching across 43,754 genes. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The resulting `data/cluster_id_mapping.csv` supports joins from BERDL pangenome annotations, including eggNOG and other functional predictions, to ADP1 genes. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

The mapping demonstrates that gene-to-cluster junction tables can serve as interoperability infrastructure between independently generated pangenomes. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] It connects [[entities/acinetobacter-baylyi-adp1]] data to the broader [[entities/berdl]] pangenome collections.

The AlphaFold analysis illustrates a second integration pattern: joining pangenome class, UniProt identifiers, MSA depth, and domain annotations. The successful join covered 38,051,842 gene-cluster–UniProt pairs, enabling comparison of conservation class with [[concepts/msa-depth]], [[concepts/structural-novelty]], and annotation richness. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]

The BERDL Atlas provides a complementary, sample-validated structural bridge. FitnessBrowser initially appeared to expose `protein_id`, but SQL probing showed that it instead uses `(orgId, locusId)` to connect `genefitness` to `besthitswissprot`; the SwissProt accession then connects to `alphafold_entries`. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

```text
genefitness --(orgId, locusId)--> besthitswissprot
besthitswissprot.sprotAccession --(UniProt accession)--> alphafold_entries
```

The live-cluster join produced 55,454 genes across 48 organisms with both fitness measurements and an AlphaFold model, representing 22,303 distinct AlphaFold models. SwissProt-best-hit coverage in AlphaFold was 99.5% (78,753 of 79,180 best hits). [src: berdl_data_atlas] This makes structure–phenotype integration a validated bridge, while the pangenome-to-AlphaFold MSA analysis remains limited to 28.7% of all gene clusters because of identifier and reference-coverage constraints. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation, berdl_data_atlas]

The environmental resistome analysis illustrates a third pattern: aggregating gene-cluster observations to species profiles and linking those profiles to categorical environments, phylogenetic strata, and AlphaEarth embeddings. Species-level environment classifications covered 95% of the 27,700 species in the broader collection, while 2,659 species had both AMR data and continuous environmental embeddings. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The Atlas identifies AlphaEarth environment embeddings indexed to 83,287 KBase genomes and places them alongside NMDC, ENIGMA, MicrobeAtlas, USGS, NETL, and Planet Microbe sample layers. [src: berdl_data_atlas] These resources create a broad genome–environment integration surface, but most connections remain candidate bridges until identifier overlap, value-space compatibility, and biological semantics are tested. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

The NMDC analysis demonstrates how a taxonomy bridge can be extended beyond genome-level annotation into community ecology. Centrifuge taxa were mapped to GTDB species-clade identifiers, and species-level GapMind scores were aggregated into community pathway-completeness measures. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The resulting scores were linked to LC-MS metabolite intensities through normalized sample-level matrices, with compound matching corrected after an isoleucine/leucine substring collision. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The NMDC bridge exposed a key coverage limitation: all 33 Freshwater samples lacked paired metabolomics and were retained for pathway-only ecosystem PCA but excluded from the Black Queen analysis. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Consequently, the observed pathway–metabolite association is effectively a soil result, whereas ecosystem separation includes Soil, Freshwater, and Unknown samples. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The BacDive bridge follows the same layered strategy but uses taxonomy as the initial key. Exact species agreement linked 33,535 strains (34.5%), and removing GTDB suffixes recovered 8,692 additional strains (8.9%). [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The phenotype-tolerance bridge retained a coverage waterfall by feature. Matched-strain counts ranged from 475 for acetate utilization to 16,454 for metabolite breadth, and species-level metal-score coverage ranged from 422 species for acetate utilization to 4,531 for isolation source. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The resulting `species_phenotype_matrix.csv` contains 5,647 species-level records, but only 3,994 species had at least five phenotype features for multivariate modelling. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The strain-variation analysis extends this pattern by retaining genome-level AMR matrices within species. It analyzed 37,444 AMR gene-species records and generated 1,305 genome-by-AMR matrices, 1,517 resistance-island records, 1,261 ANI-based Mantel results, 974 ecotype summaries, and 513 temporal-regression results. [src: amr_strain_variation]

The AMR fitness analysis joined pangenome-derived AMR classes and conservation labels to Fitness Browser gene identifiers and experiment metadata, producing 801 per-gene non-antibiotic fitness summaries, 25 organism-level effect sizes, and 954 antibiotic-validation measurements. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The annotation-gap study illustrates reaction-level evidence triangulation across organisms. EC-based matching connected 201 gapfilled reaction–organism pairs to pangenome gene clusters and fitness evidence; Bakta supplied alternative EC numbers and product-name matches; pangenome presence/absence and fitness specificity supplied cross-organism context; and DIAMOND searches against Swiss-Prot exemplars supplied sequence evidence. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The full pipeline resolved 96 pairs (47.8%), including 44 high-confidence, 19 medium-confidence, and 33 low-confidence assignments. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

EC matching alone resolved 51 pairs (25.4%), Bakta alone resolved 22 (10.9%), and BLAST alone resolved 70 (34.8%), whereas the full pipeline resolved 96 (47.8%). [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Leave-one-out results ranged from 73 resolved pairs without BLAST to 86 without EC matching, showing that the evidence streams contribute overlapping but non-identical information. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

Together, these bridges establish a layered integration model: relational tables resolve identifier incompatibilities; pangenome classes provide conservation context; genome-level matrices retain strain variation; reaction- and pathway-level tables preserve biochemical context; and phenotype, phylogenetic, environmental, structural, isolation, experimental, community, and metabolomic tables add biological interpretation. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, alphafold_msa_annotation, amr_environmental_resistome, amr_fitness_cost, amr_strain_variation, annotation_gap_discovery, bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology, berdl_data_atlas]

## BERDL cross-tenant integration surface

The BERDL Data Atlas places pangenome integration within a lakehouse-wide network rather than treating it as isolated pairwise joins. The catalog contains 536 unordered bridges at the tenant-by-topic level, with some bridges sharing as many as seven canonical keys. Examples include `kbase.pathway` with `kescience.pathway`, `kescience.pathway` with `phagefoundry.mobile_phage`, and `refdata.structural` with `kescience.structural` through `alphafold_pdb`, `pfam`, and `protein_id`. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

Cross-tenant reuse is substantial: 51 of 66 audited BERIL projects (77%) span multiple tenants. The `kbase` tenant occurs in 53 of 66 projects (80%), `kescience` in 35 of 66 (53%), and the realized `kbase`–`kescience` bridge accounts for 36 cross-tenant projects. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

Five high-leverage bridges had zero realized use at audit time: `kescience`–`refdata` for structural fitness, `enigma`–`phagefoundry` for subsurface prophages and metal resistance, `kbase`–`refdata` for GTDB and pangenome reconciliation, `nmdc`–`protect` for environmental pathogen context, and `nmdc`–`refdata` for ENVO annotation coverage. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The FitnessBrowser–AlphaFold bridge is sample-validated, whereas the other proposed use cases remain hypotheses requiring live-cluster tests of identifier overlap and biological value compatibility. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

Data volume and integration use are not equivalent. ENIGMA holds 36% of BERDL tables but appeared in only six audited projects, while PhageFoundry holds 14% of tables and appeared in five projects. DOE-BER provides approximately 63% of tables, covers all 15 mapped biological topics, and supplies the main backbone for realized cross-tenant analyses. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

For pangenome work, source selection should begin with the biological layer—genome, phenotype, environment, structure, pathway, phage, pathogen, or literature—and then inspect the canonical bridge exposed by both sources. [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] provides a principal genome reference; FitnessBrowser provides gene-level fitness; BacDive provides organismal or growth phenotypes; AlphaFold, PDB, UniProt, and UniRef provide structural and protein references; and ENIGMA, PROTECT, PhageFoundry, Planet Microbe, NETL, and USGS provide specialized field or genome collections. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

This heuristic does not replace validation. A shared `ncbi_taxon_id` may support a taxonomic join, while a shared `genome_id` or `protein_id` requires inspection of identifier provenance, cardinality, null rates, versioning, and representative value overlap before interpretation. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

## Biological context

The relevant *Acinetobacter baylyi* species clade included 13 genomes, with 3,207 core and 1,684 accessory gene clusters. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Essential genes were more likely to belong to the core pangenome. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

The deletion-collection analysis showed that pangenome status is associated with experimental coverage among TnSeq-dispensable genes. Of 2,593 TnSeq-dispensable genes, 272 (10.5%) lacked deletion-collection growth data; these genes were less often pangenome-core than represented dispensable genes (76.5% versus 93.3%, p = 1.4×10⁻²⁰), were shorter on average, less frequently annotated, and enriched for hypothetical or unannotated proteins. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] This supports a hypothesis that less-conserved and poorly annotated genes are disproportionately vulnerable to incomplete deletion-collection representation, without establishing accessory status as the cause of missingness. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

Among 2,034 genes measured across all eight carbon sources, 625 (31%) had condition-specificity scores ≥ 1.0. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] The quinate-specific module included aromatic-degradation genes and NADH–ubiquinone oxidoreductase subunits, creating an opportunity to compare condition-specific dependencies with core or accessory status across related genomes. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

The BacDive integration provides direct ecological validation of pangenome-derived metal scores. Heavy-metal contamination isolates had a normalized metal-tolerance score one standard deviation above the environmental baseline (Cohen's d = +1.00, Mann–Whitney p = 0.006, n = 10); the baseline median was 0.187 and mean was 0.195. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The association also appeared for waste/sludge (d = +0.57), all contamination (d = +0.43), industrial (d = +0.20), and host-associated (d = +0.14), with p < 0.0001 for each comparison except the heavy-metal result. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The phenotype-tolerance analysis qualifies what ecological validation can establish. Gram-negative species had higher composite metal-tolerance scores than Gram-positive species (Cohen's d = -0.61, p < 1e-60, n = 3,272), but the association was almost entirely between-lineage and could not be tested within taxonomic classes. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Oxygen tolerance showed no biologically meaningful association: the anaerobe–aerobe contrast was d = -0.016, p = 0.55. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

Seven of ten phenotype features passed FDR correction, including Gram stain, oxidase, motility, urease, enzyme breadth, nitrate reduction, and catalase. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] However, taxonomy alone explained 35.4% of metal-score variance, phenotype features alone explained 16.3%, and taxonomy plus phenotype yielded R² = 0.345, slightly below taxonomy alone. Adding `n_metal_clusters` produced R² = 0.633. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The phenotype results therefore provide a direct example of [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding]] in pangenome integration. The Gram-negative association is mechanistically plausible because the outer membrane can restrict metal-cation uptake, but the available data cannot distinguish that mechanism from lineage membership. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Urease-positive species had lower overall metal scores (d = -0.18), driven by Actinomycetes (d = -0.59, p < 1e-16); the effect disappeared in Gammaproteobacteria and Bacilli. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This suggests that nickel handling associated with [[entities/urease-complex]] may be specific to nickel homeostasis rather than a general metal-tolerance mechanism. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The contamination signal persisted within Pseudomonadota (delta = +0.040, p < 0.001) and Actinomycetota (delta = +0.035, p < 0.001), but not within Bacillota or Bacteroidota. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This supports a genome–ecology relationship beyond broad phylum composition in the two best-sampled phyla, while null results may reflect biological differences or limited power. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The annotation-gap study found that baseline FBA across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations had 42.5% overall accuracy, with 86.5% recall and 42.5% precision; conditional gapfilling for 38 false-negative cases added 219 reactions, including 201 enzymatic reactions. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Of those 201 reaction–organism pairs, 96 (47.8%) received candidate genes through integrated evidence. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

Resolution ranged from 20% in *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* to 71.4% in *Klebsiella michiganensis*. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The low rate for *B. thetaiotaomicron* suggests a hypothesis that phylogenetic divergence and divergent metabolism reduce transferability of reference annotations, but 12 of 14 organisms were Proteobacteria, limiting generalization. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

Of 201 gapfilled reactions, 50 (24.9%) lacked an EC number in ModelSEED and only 8 of those 50 (16%) were resolved, compared with 88 of 151 (58.3%) reactions with known EC numbers. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] These EC-less reactions connect [[concepts/annotation-gap]] with [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and represent cases where stoichiometric evidence is available but enzyme classification is not. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

GapMind pathway predictions partially corroborated ModelSEED gapfilling for 104 pathway pairings, but exact concordance was limited because GapMind reports pathway-level completeness and step counts rather than the individual step identities available in gapfill output. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] This illustrates [[concepts/method-concordance]] being constrained by dataset granularity.

The NMDC results extended this metabolic-integration logic from organisms to communities. PCA of the 220-sample × 80-pathway completeness matrix separated Soil and Freshwater communities: PC1 explained 49.4% of variance, PC2 explained 16.6%, and PC1–5 explained 83.0%. Ecosystem differences were significant on PC1 (Kruskal-Wallis H = 52.98, p < 0.0001) and PC2 (H = 123.74, p < 0.0001); median PC1 was +3.86 for Soil and −6.28 for Freshwater. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

PC1 was dominated by carbon-utilization pathways, including glucuronate, fumarate, succinate, lysine, and cellobiose, while amino-acid pathways loaded more strongly on PC2. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Seventeen of 18 amino-acid pathways differed significantly across ecosystem types after FDR correction; tyrosine was the only non-significant pathway (q = 0.71). [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

Across 13 testable amino-acid pathways, 11 (85%) had negative Spearman correlations with ambient metabolite intensity; the sign pattern was non-random (binomial p = 0.011). Leucine was significant (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62), as was arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80). Methionine had the largest effect (r = −0.496, n = 18, q = 0.117) but lacked FDR significance. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

This provides weak but consistent support for [[concepts/black-queen-dynamics]] at community scale and illustrates [[concepts/metabolite-production-utilization-decoupling]] and [[concepts/capability-versus-kinetics]]. Community pathway completeness measures gene-encoded potential, while metabolite intensity reflects production, consumption, transport, persistence, and measurement. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The AMR fitness-cost analysis found that AMR-gene knockouts had higher relative fitness than non-AMR knockouts under non-antibiotic conditions in all 25 tested organisms. A DerSimonian–Laird random-effects meta-analysis estimated a pooled shift of +0.086 [95% CI: +0.074, +0.098], z = 14.3, p approximately 0, with median per-organism Cohen's d = 0.18. Under antibiotic exposure, 57.0% of AMR genes showed a fitness flip toward greater importance across 797 gene–antibiotic observations. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Core and accessory AMR genes had virtually identical non-antibiotic fitness distributions (mean −0.024 in each group, Cohen's d = 0.002, Mann–Whitney p = 0.33), showing that pangenome conservation does not necessarily predict residual metabolic cost. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This links [[concepts/shared-dispensability]] and [[concepts/condition-dependent-essentiality]] to comparative pangenome analysis without treating experimental dispensability and accessory status as interchangeable classifications.

Across 1,305 species and 180,025 genomes, 51.3% of AMR gene-species occurrences were rare, 41.3% variable, and 7.5% fixed; the median variability index was 0.526 and median pairwise strain AMR Jaccard distance was 0.435. [src: amr_strain_variation] Atlas conservation class predicted strain-level prevalence: 77.3% of Core AMR genes were fixed, 57.3% of Auxiliary genes were variable, and 78.7% of Singleton genes were rare. [src: amr_strain_variation] These results strengthen [[concepts/core-accessory-resistance]].

The same analysis detected 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species, with mean island size 6.2 genes, median size 4, maximum size 43, and mean phi = 0.827. Eighty-eight percent contained genes from multiple resistance mechanisms. [src: amr_strain_variation] Co-occurrence is evidence of linkage rather than proof of co-selection or functional synergy. [src: amr_strain_variation]

Of 1,261 species tested with ANI-based Mantel analyses, 701 (55.6%) had significant signal after FDR correction, the median Mantel r was 0.247, and 87.8% showed positive correlation. [src: amr_strain_variation] Non-core AMR genes had stronger signal than core genes (median r = 0.222 versus 0.117; paired t-test t = −8.35, p = 7.0e−16, n = 489). [src: amr_strain_variation] This strengthens [[concepts/phylogenetic-amr-structure]] and suggests that acquired resistance may often be maintained within lineages after acquisition, although the analysis does not distinguish vertical inheritance from repeated transfer among close relatives. [src: amr_strain_variation]

At broader scale, clinical and human-gut species had a median of 5 AMR gene clusters, compared with 2 for soil, aquatic, and host-associated species (Kruskal–Wallis H = 781.9, p = 9.4×10⁻¹⁶⁷, eta² = 0.056). Clinical species averaged 67.6% accessory AMR and human-gut species 80.3%, compared with 42.9% in soil species. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The AlphaFold results show that conservation class is associated with sequence representation. Core clusters had median MSA depth 15,308, compared with 5,299 for auxiliary-plus-singleton clusters and 5,527 for auxiliary non-singletons; the 10th-percentile depth was 334 for core genes versus 25–32 for accessory classes. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Across 38,051,842 gene-cluster–UniProt pairs, MSA depth correlated with domain annotation richness (Spearman rho = 0.7563); mean domain hits increased from 0.59 at depth <10 to 10.83 at depth ≥10,000. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] This is an integration route rather than evidence that MSA depth causes annotation quality.

The pangenome-openness result qualifies these biological associations at the level of a global summary metric. Openness did not predict environment or phylogeny effect sizes, despite the corpus containing stronger relationships for particular gene classes, resistance profiles, or ecological metadata. [src: pangenome_openness] The most defensible interpretation is that pangenome openness is not a universal proxy for ecological adaptation, HGT directionality, or phylogenetic dominance. Alternative openness measures, functional stratification, and lifestyle interactions are needed before treating the null as general across all pangenomes. [src: pangenome_openness]

## Evidence and limitations

The ADP1 cluster bridge is complete at the reported gene level but indirect and dependent on three tables. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Cluster splits, merges, or ambiguous assignments could create edge cases, so successful mapping does not prove identical clustering parameters or biological definitions. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

The Atlas's 536 bridges describe schema connectivity, not universal biological interoperability. Only the FitnessBrowser–AlphaFold bridge was sample-validated, and proposed bridges require direct execution before their biological questions can be treated as findings. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Across-tenant deduplication was not performed, and identifiers such as `genome_id` may differ in meaning among KBase, NCBI, MAG, and other pipelines. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

Environmental analyses depend on majority-vote species labels, uneven clinical and environmental sampling, and incomplete metadata. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Species-level clinical fraction is a proxy rather than a true within-species per-genome comparison, and 18.7% of AMR clusters lacked mechanism assignments. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The strain-variation analysis found that 52.7% of genomes lacked a classifiable isolation source. [src: amr_strain_variation] BacDive adds a related limitation: 56.6% of strains lacked a matched GTDB species in the phenotype bridge, and only 10 heavy-metal isolates remained after matching. [src: bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

AMR detection depends on AMRFinderPlus and may miss novel mechanisms. [src: amr_fitness_cost, amr_strain_variation] Fitness data came from lab-adapted strains; 86% of AMR genes were Tier 2 keyword matches; antibiotic validation covered only four matched resistance classes; and transposon insertions may have polar effects. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Core/accessory interpretation is sensitive to genome sampling. Fitness Browser species had a median of 9 GTDB genomes, ranging from 2–399, so a gene present in a small sample can be labelled core despite being accessory at larger sampling depth. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This limitation also affects core-versus-non-core AMR comparisons because near-universal core prevalence inherently reduces distance variation. [src: amr_fitness_cost, amr_strain_variation]

The metal-tolerance score is genome-size-normalized but may still correlate with total metabolic complexity. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Metal Fitness Atlas scores are genome-based predictions rather than direct metal-tolerance measurements, so phenotype associations are phenotype-to-genome correlations. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The metabolic annotation-gap integration is limited by overly permissive automated models, non-unique ModelSEED gapfilling, manual compound-name mapping, partial GapMind coverage, and circular knockout validation because gapfilled reactions were required for growth on the tested media. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The dataset was also phylogenetically biased: 12 of 14 organisms were Proteobacteria. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

The NMDC integration lacked paired metabolomics for all 33 Freshwater samples, lacked abiotic variables for the 174-sample analysis matrix, and relied mainly on names because KEGG identifiers were present for only 2% of metabolomics records. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Approximately 6.5% of mapped abundance came from genus-proxy-ambiguous taxa resolved by an alphabetical GTDB-clade tiebreaker, and 125 of 131 samples in the H1 dataset (95%) originated from one study. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The pangenome-openness analysis has its own limitations: the sample includes only species with both pangenome statistics and ecotype results; openness is a single summary metric; environment and phylogeny effects are derived from partial correlations that may not fully disentangle confounding; and some ecotype analyses may have limited power because of few genomes per species. [src: pangenome_openness] The null correlations therefore constrain the tested dataset and metrics rather than proving that pangenome structure is biologically independent of ecology or phylogeny in all settings. [src: pangenome_openness]

The AlphaFold bridge covered only 29.3% of all clusters and was biased toward better-characterised organisms. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] MSA depth was retrieved for representative sequences rather than all within-cluster diversity, and the reported correlation was not stratified by organism or pangenome class. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]

Finally, the integration results are observational. Resistance-island co-occurrence does not prove co-selection, environment-associated mechanism differences do not establish ecological causality, phylogenetic signal in non-core genes does not distinguish vertical inheritance from repeated transfer, and a triangulated metabolic candidate, community pathway score, genome-derived metal score, or pangenome openness association does not constitute experimental confirmation. [src: amr_strain_variation, amr_environmental_resistome, annotation_gap_discovery, bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology, pangenome_openness]

## Tensions

Pangenome conservation is informative but not uniformly predictive of phenotype or function. Core status is associated with essentiality and higher experimental representation in ADP1, and core AMR genes are more often fixed within species. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, adp1_deletion_phenotypes, amr_strain_variation] However, core and accessory AMR genes had virtually identical non-antibiotic fitness distributions, many conserved low-MSA-depth core clusters remained hypothetical, and phenotype features did not improve taxonomy-based metal-tolerance prediction. [src: amr_fitness_cost, alphafold_msa_annotation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The openness analysis adds that a species-level open/closed classification did not predict either environment or phylogeny effect size (rho = -0.05, p = 0.54; rho = 0.03, p = 0.73). [src: pangenome_openness] Conservation and openness should therefore be treated as contextual evidence rather than universal proxies for essentiality, fitness cost, annotation quality, ecological exposure, HGT directionality, or functional certainty.

Cross-organism evidence transfer improves metabolic annotation overall, but resolution varies 3.5-fold across organisms and is only 16% for EC-less dark reactions. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] This creates a tension between the value of pangenome-based transfer and the risk that phylogenetic distance, divergent metabolism, or missing EC labels make transferred evidence unreliable. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

Species-level environmental patterns are statistically strong in broad resistome and BacDive analyses, whereas within-species environment–ecotype tests and metal-utilization comparisons are limited by missing metadata or small matched samples. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_strain_variation, bacdive_metal_validation] The contrast may reflect differences in scale, sampling, matching success, and metadata completeness rather than contradictory biology.

The NMDC analysis creates a scale tension. Community pathway completeness separated ecosystems strongly and showed a majority-negative relationship with metabolite intensity, but H1 lacked Freshwater samples, abiotic covariates, and expression measurements. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Community genomic potential can therefore be associated with chemistry without demonstrating that pathway loss, rather than habitat or metabolite consumption, drives the relationship.

The BacDive phenotype results introduce a tension between biologically plausible traits and predictive independence. Several phenotype features were individually associated with metal scores, yet taxonomy plus phenotype performed slightly worse than taxonomy alone (R² = 0.345 versus 0.354), while metal-resistance gene-cluster count improved the full model to R² = 0.633. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Phenotypes may capture real lineage-level biology without adding information beyond phylogeny and genome-encoded resistance repertoire.

The BERDL Atlas adds a systems-level tension between theoretical and realized integration. There are 536 schema-level cross-tenant bridges and 77% of audited projects use multiple tenants, yet high-leverage bridges involving reference data, NMDC, ENIGMA, and PhageFoundry had no realized use at audit time. [src: berdl_data_atlas] This gap may reflect documentation, coordination, or validation costs rather than a lack of biological value; the validated FitnessBrowser–AlphaFold use case demonstrates that a promising recipe can require correction before execution. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

## Open Directions

- Test pangenome openness against alternative metrics, including auxiliary fraction, Heap's law alpha, and pangenome fluidity, using the same merged ecotype dataset. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Stratify openness analyses by gene function, especially L (mobile) and V (defense) categories, to determine whether a global null masks functional effects. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Test openness × lifestyle interactions, including open pathogens versus open environmental species, and quantify power within each stratum. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Audit one-to-many and many-to-one patterns in the ADP1 three-table bridge to identify cluster splits, merges, and ambiguous assignments. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]
- Use genome-accession matching between BacDive and pangenome genome tables to recover strains missed by species-name matching and reassess contamination and phenotype effects. [src: bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Increase the number of heavy-metal isolates and repeat the power analysis to estimate the ecological effect more precisely. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Apply PGLS or phylogenetic PCA to remove shared ancestry formally before testing phenotype–metal associations. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Test whether the ADP1 quinate-specific module and other carbon-source modules are core or accessory across related genomes. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]
- Compare missing dispensable genes with cluster length, annotation status, multiplicity, and conservation to determine whether missingness persists after controlling for gene properties. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]
- Recompute MSA-depth/domain-richness associations separately for core, auxiliary non-singleton, and auxiliary-plus-singleton clusters. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]
- Map low-depth core clusters onto the GTDB phylogeny and test whether they are concentrated in taxonomically undersampled lineages. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]
- Use the validated FitnessBrowser–SwissProt–AlphaFold route to test whether high-fitness-impact genes share structural features, adding per-residue confidence where available. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
- Validate proposed GTDB–pangenome, ENIGMA–PhageFoundry, NMDC–PROTECT, and NMDC–reference-data bridges using live-cluster identifier overlap and value-space checks. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
- Build automated bridge-quality checks for key semantics, null rates, cardinality, version compatibility, and representative value overlap before publishing cross-tenant joins. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
- Reanalyze environment-associated AMR patterns within adequately sampled families and species using genome-level environmental labels, phylogeny, and sampling covariates. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_strain_variation]
- Map strain-level resistance islands to plasmids, chromosomes, integrons, and insertion sequences to distinguish physical linkage from functional co-selection. [src: amr_strain_variation]
- Recalculate AMR core/accessory fitness comparisons within species with at least 20 genomes to test whether the pooled null result persists when conservation labels are better resolved. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Extend metabolic gap-resolution analysis from 14 organisms to all 48 Fitness Browser organisms and compare ModelSEED with gapseq reconstructions. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Experimentally test the 44 high-confidence reaction–gene assignments, prioritizing rxn02185 and rxn03436 across the nine organisms in which each reaction was repeatedly resolved. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Apply enzyme-prediction methods to the 50 EC-less reactions and compare predictions with pangenome conservation, fitness specificity, Bakta alternatives, and sequence homology. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Obtain or generate metabolomics for the 33 Freshwater NMDC samples to test whether community pathway–metabolite relationships differ between aquatic and terrestrial communities. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Replicate the leucine and arginine analyses across additional NMDC studies with study-level random effects and abiotic covariates. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Pair metatranscriptomics with metabolomics and community pathway completeness to distinguish expressed biosynthesis from genomic potential. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Improve KEGG compound annotation and resolve Unknown ecosystem labels using ENVO and study metadata to expand pathway tests and refine habitat comparisons. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Compare urease-positive and urease-negative strains within the same taxonomic class using per-metal fitness profiling, especially nickel versus non-nickel stress. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Refresh the BERDL inventory and bridge atlas at major lakehouse ingest milestones because table counts, identifier coverage, and realized project reuse will change as collections are added. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
