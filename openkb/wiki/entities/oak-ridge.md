# Oak Ridge

Oak Ridge is the location of groundwater sites analyzed for relationships between uranium contamination, microbial community composition, and laboratory fitness measurements. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Relevance to the Report

The study analyzed 108 Oak Ridge groundwater samples using geochemistry data and 16S amplicon community profiles from [[entities/enigma-coral]]. [src: lab_field_ecology] Fourteen of 26 genera represented in the [[entities/fitness-browser]] were detected at these sites. [src: lab_field_ecology]

The most prevalent detected genera were [[entities/sphingomonas]] (93% of sites), [[entities/pseudomonas]] (91%), and [[entities/caulobacter]] (82%). [src: lab_field_ecology] [[entities/desulfovibrio]], an ENIGMA model organism associated with uranium reduction, was detected at 34% of sites and reached a maximum relative abundance of 0.09%. [src: lab_field_ecology]

Five of 11 sufficiently prevalent Fitness Browser genera showed significant associations with uranium after false-discovery-rate correction: [[entities/herbaspirillum]] and [[entities/bacteroides]] were more abundant at high-uranium sites, whereas [[entities/caulobacter]], [[entities/sphingomonas]], and [[entities/pedobacter]] were less abundant. [src: lab_field_ecology] These results support [[concepts/environmental-metal-tolerance]] and [[concepts/subsurface-microbial-specialization]], while also showing that field community structure cannot be explained by laboratory metal tolerance alone. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Ecological Context

Oak Ridge sites differ across multiple environmental dimensions, including pH, redox potential, carbon sources, sulfate, nitrate, and uranium concentration. [src: lab_field_ecology] The report therefore interprets contamination-associated community shifts as broader ecological restructuring rather than a simple enrichment of metal-tolerant organisms. [src: lab_field_ecology]

The report identifies [[entities/rhodanobacter]] as a priority for future Fitness Browser profiling because prior work describes it as dominant in contaminated Oak Ridge wells, while it is absent from the current Fitness Browser comparison. [src: lab_field_ecology]
