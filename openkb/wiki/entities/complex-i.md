# Complex I

## Identity

- **Canonical name:** Complex I
- **Aliases:** NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase; NADH dehydrogenase; NDH-1
- **Entity type:** gene_or_pathway

Complex I is a multisubunit respiratory complex that reoxidizes NADH generated by central metabolism. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Role in ADP1 Aromatic Catabolism

Complex I is the largest support subsystem in the 51-gene [[entities/acinetobacter-baylyi-adp1|ADP1]] quinate-catabolism network, accounting for 21 genes (41%) associated with quinate-specific growth phenotypes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

Aromatic degradation converts quinate through protocatechuate and β-ketoadipate into succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA, which enter the TCA cycle and generate NADH; Complex I provides the respiratory capacity needed to reoxidize this NADH under high pathway flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

FBA predicted 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates than on the comparison substrates, with flux values of 0.55 versus 0.31, but predicted 0% essentiality for Complex I genes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This discrepancy is an example of [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling|metabolic-model limitations]] and [[concepts/condition-dependent-essentiality|condition-dependent essentiality]]: the model represented increased flux demand but did not capture the bottleneck created by loss of a complete multisubunit complex. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The Complex I operon spans 714–729 kb and contains 13 nuo subunits, nuoA–nuoN, located on the same strand with intergenic distances below 100 bp. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Ten of these 13 subunits independently produced quinate-specific growth defects, consistent with disruption of one subunit eliminating complex function. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## NADH-Flux Interpretation

Cross-species ortholog-transferred fitness data showed significantly poorer Complex I ortholog fitness on aromatic conditions than on non-aromatic conditions, with means of −1.35 and −0.77, respectively, and a Mann–Whitney p-value below 0.0001. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The strongest Complex I defects relative to background occurred on acetate (−1.55) and succinate (−1.39), despite those substrates being non-aromatic. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] These results support the [[concepts/nadh-flux-respiratory-constraints|NADH-flux respiratory-constraint]] interpretation: Complex I dependence tracks high NADH production through the TCA cycle rather than aromatic chemistry alone. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The report proposes that [[entities/ndh-2|NDH-2]] compensates for Complex I on simpler or lower-NADH substrates, including glucose and lactate, while lacking sufficient capacity under high NADH flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This remains a hypothesis requiring direct ADP1 tests. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Candidate Accessory Factors

Co-fitness analysis assigned 11 non-core genes to Complex I-associated functions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] ACIAD3137, annotated as UPF0234, and ACIAD2176, annotated as DUF2280, each showed correlation greater than 0.98 with Complex I genes and are candidate uncharacterized Complex I accessory factors. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

These assignments are based on phenotypic correlation rather than demonstrated physical association, so they may represent indirect respiratory or regulatory connections. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The finding illustrates the use of [[concepts/cofitness-networks|co-fitness networks]] to resolve genes missed by conventional annotation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Model and Experimental Context

Thirty of the 51 quinate-specific genes lacked FBA reaction mappings, including Complex I-associated support genes, PQQ and iron supply functions, and regulatory genes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This demonstrates that [[entities/flux-balance-analysis|flux-balance analysis]] captures core metabolic flux but may omit respiratory-chain capacity, cofactor supply, and regulatory infrastructure. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The report recommends testing the Complex I–NDH-2 division of labor by identifying ADP1 NDH-2 genes and comparing their deletion phenotypes on quinate, glucose, acetate, and succinate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] It also recommends expanding the condition panel with additional aromatic substrates, iron limitation, and respiratory inhibitors. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Related Biology

Complex I supports the [[entities/quinate-aromatic-degradation|quinate/aromatic degradation]] pathway by handling the NADH burden produced during β-ketoadipate catabolism. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The pathway also depends on [[entities/pqq-biosynthesis|PQQ biosynthesis]] for quinate dehydrogenase and [[entities/protocatechuate-3-4-dioxygenase|protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase]], which requires [[entities/iron|iron]] for aromatic-ring cleavage. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
