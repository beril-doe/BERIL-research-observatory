# Sphingomonas

## Identity

Sphingomonas is a bacterial genus associated with aromatic-compound catabolism. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Findings in lignin enrichment

Sphingomonas was more abundant in the unenriched base community (1.74%) than in the lignin-enriched treatments. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Its decline after enrichment suggests that known aromatic-catabolic capacity did not translate into selection under the experimental lignin conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

This result qualifies a simple capability-based interpretation of enrichment: possessing pathways associated with aromatic degradation does not necessarily imply activity, competitive success, or increased abundance in a particular environment. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Interpretation and limitations

The study measured relative abundance from 16S amplicon sequencing rather than lignin transformation rates, gene expression, enzyme activity, or absolute cell abundance. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Therefore, the observed decline indicates reduced relative representation in enriched communities, but it does not establish that Sphingomonas cannot metabolize lignin-derived compounds or that its absolute population decreased. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

The finding relates to [[concepts/capability-versus-kinetics]] and [[concepts/environmental-occupancy-vs-activity]]: genomic or literature-based metabolic potential should be distinguished from demonstrated activity and enrichment under the tested conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
