# Review: Gene and Protein Function Annotations in BERDL, with Coordinates — and SemCat as the Instrument That Finds Them

## Summary

This is a well-designed, dual-purpose project — a census of BERDL's gene/protein annotation tables plus an independent instrument evaluation of the semantic catalog (SemCat) used to discover them — and the methodological scaffolding is genuinely strong: a non-circular design (NB01 freezes the SemCat-only answer before NB02 builds ground truth), discriminating queries run and reported first in every notebook, a pre-registered decision rule applied honestly (including a correction that moved the headline verdict from H3 to H2, disclosed rather than hidden), and a Scope/Limitations/"What Would Have Changed Our Mind" apparatus that is unusually candid for this kind of report. However, close inspection of the committed artifacts surfaces a real reproducibility problem: `02_ground_truth_sweep.ipynb` was re-executed on 2026-10-06 — four days after `01`, `03`, and `04` were run on 2026-10-02 — and because it re-reads live catalog state top-to-bottom, the re-run silently changed the ground-truth table/column counts and the SemCat coverage numbers. The downstream notebooks (`03`, `04`) and `REPORT.md` were never refreshed to match, so the repository as committed is internally inconsistent: `REPORT.md`'s own quoted coverage figures, and even its "thirteen tables failed the sweep" limitation, are contradicted by the data files sitting next to it. Separately, a pseudoreplication correction the research plan explicitly pre-registered (deduplicating by `schema_sig` to avoid NMDC's cloned staging tables inflating apparent sample size) was never implemented in the executed notebooks. Neither issue appears to invalidate the qualitative conclusions, but both should be fixed before this is presented as a frozen, reproducible artifact.

## Methodology

The research question is clearly stated and genuinely falsifiable: Q1 (census) is enumeration and is reported regardless of outcome; Q2 (is SemCat a sufficient discovery instrument?) carries four competing, mutually exclusive hypotheses (H0–H3) with explicit predictions, falsification tests, and numeric decision thresholds fixed in advance. This is a good allocation — the plan correctly resists turning the enumeration task into a fake hypothesis and instead puts the pre-registration effort where real uncertainty exists.

The discrimination strategy is sound: the coordinate-system sub-test and the 200-column value-adjudicated audit (100 precision-stratum, 100 recall-stratum, SEED-reproducible) are run and reported *before* the verdict is computed, and the ordering constraint ("01 must run before 02, because it freezes the SemCat-only answer before the independent ground truth exists") is correctly identified as load-bearing and is followed.

`beril.yaml` records `plan_approval.via: agent-relayed` — the approval was recorded by the agent from an in-conversation user confirmation, not witnessed by the CLI at a terminal. This is disclosed context for a reader weighing the pre-registration claim, not itself a fault.

**Reproducibility — the README's reproduction instructions are good in principle but the repo does not currently satisfy them.** The README is unusually careful about tiering (on-cluster only, `q.py` not `run_sql.py`), gives a runtime estimate, and explicitly anticipates that "01 and 02 read live state" and that "the stale/missing table counts (159/185) will change as NMDC staging databases rotate." That caveat is the right instinct, but it undersells what has already happened inside this repository (see Findings Assessment below): the drift it warns about for *future* re-runs has already occurred *between* notebooks in the current commit, and the published report was not reconciled against it.

## Code Quality

The `src/` split (`semcat.py` for shared regexes and the coordinate classifier, `census.py` for the curated, hand-adjudicated table list with a per-row `basis` field, `validators.py` for value-based label checking) is a good design — it stops the four notebooks from silently drifting on what "ground truth" or "coordinate system" means, and the validators correctly treat vacuous labels (`identifier`, `annotation_text`, etc.) as unfalsifiable rather than scoring them as hits.

Query practice is careful: `LIMIT`-pushdown value sampling instead of `DISTINCT` on billion-row tables (`03_semcat_grade.ipynb` cell 8), Iceberg metadata counts instead of trusting `row_count` blindly, and a biome-partition-aware join in `04_bgc_region_joinability.ipynb` rather than a full scan. The project's own `memories/pitfalls.md` is a genuinely useful, non-redundant set of six findings (the `run_sql.py` off-cluster trap, `content_type='coordinate'` conflation, `get_db_structure()` returning a JSON string, variable shadowing that silently corrupted a published metric, pandas attribute/column collisions) and the notebooks visibly apply the lessons (e.g., `fn_df` renamed in NB02 §4 with a comment explaining why, after the `fn`-shadowing pitfall was caught).

One gap: the research plan's unit-of-analysis decision commits to computing every rate "twice, once per column and once per distinct `schema_sig`... the schema_sig figure is the one quoted" where they disagree (`RESEARCH_PLAN.md`, Competing Hypotheses section, and repeated in the Pre-registered Decision Rule). `schema_sig` is selected into a dataframe once (`01_semcat_discovery.ipynb` cell 17) but never used to deduplicate anything, and no per-`schema_sig` rate appears anywhere in `03_semcat_grade.ipynb` or `REPORT.md`. The hazard this was meant to guard against is real and present in the data: NMDC keeps multiple dated staging copies of the same schema (`nmdc.nmdc_metadata_staging_20260923_58277b41...` appears as an independently-sampled row in NB03's recall stratum, cell 13/14 output), which is exactly the cloned-schema pseudoreplication the plan cites Felsenstein (1985) to justify correcting. This isn't flagged as a dropped deviation anywhere (contrast with the two deviations that *are* logged, in `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`'s Revision History v2 and the gap-median correction in `REPORT.md`).

## Evaluation Integrity

This is descriptive/instrument-evaluation work with no model fit, so train/test leakage and baseline selection (EVALUATION_INTEGRITY items 3–4) don't apply, and the report correctly doesn't force that framing.

- **Selection bias (item 1):** the ground truth itself is built by name-regex triage plus manual adjudication, and the report is explicit that this is a lower bound — `kbase.content.c_<genome>_features` (full genomic coordinates plus ontology terms, packed into a column named `location`) was missed by the project's own regex and found only by reading values. This is exactly the right way to handle it: the Limitations section states plainly that "true recall may be *lower* than 32%, not higher" as a result. Good.
- **Pseudoreplication (item 5 analog, cross-genome → here cross-*schema-clone*):** as noted under Code Quality, the pre-registered `schema_sig` deduplication was not executed, and the audit sample draws at least one NMDC staging-database clone as an independent recall-stratum row. This is the one place the project's own stated safeguard against inflated-n evidence was dropped without a logged deviation.
- **Metric misuse (item 2):** none found. The strict/lenient precision split, the exclusion of `sentinel_only` placeholder-only columns from the denominator, and the explicit argument for why the full-frame rule-based confusion matrix (§4) is a biased comparator rather than the primary result, are all handled carefully and disclosed rather than cherry-picked. The claims ledger (`claims.json`) independently flags 2 of 6 claims as single-stream with a confidence mismatch, and `REPORT.md`'s own "note on the claims ledger" section discusses both honestly rather than arguing them away — this is good practice and should be called out as a strength.
- **Effect size / large-N significance (item 11):** not really applicable here — this is a measured rate (precision/recall) on an audited sample (n=62–100), not a hypothesis test on billions of rows, and the report appropriately flags that the 52/62 precision figure has a binomial CI (~±9 points) that straddles the 0.80 threshold, rather than reporting 83.9% as more precise than it is.

## Findings Assessment

**The headline findings are each backed by numbers that are internally consistent within the notebook that produced them** — I verified `04_bgc_region_joinability.ipynb`'s cell outputs against `REPORT.md` line by line (28,494/28,494 contig overlap, 754,313 gene–BGC pairs, 13.9 genes/BGC, 6,530 co-occurring proteins, 94% domain ordering, median gap −392 aa with IQR [−412, −371]) and `03_semcat_grade.ipynb`'s audit outputs (recall 32.0%, strict precision 83.9%, lenient 91.9%, full-frame confusion 0.54/0.44/0.50) — all match exactly.

**But the project's coverage/reproducibility claims do not currently match the committed artifacts, and this is verifiable, not speculative:**

1. `REPORT.md` and `claims.json` both quote "159 stale tables, 185 missing tables, agreement 0.79" and attribute it to `notebooks/02_ground_truth_sweep.ipynb#cell-16`. The notebook file as currently committed (execution timestamp 2026-10-06 20:00, re-run after NB01/03/04's 2026-10-02 run) shows that cell outputting **5 stale, 163 missing, agreement 80%** — confirmed independently by the committed `data/semcat_coverage_diff.json`, which contains exactly 5 stale and 163 missing entries, not 159/185. `claims.json` marks this claim's evidence as `"resolved"`, but the exact text it claims to have found in cell-16 is not what that cell currently contains.
2. `REPORT.md`'s Limitations section states "Thirteen tables failed the sweep, all from one cause (a stale `s3a://cdm-lake/users-sql` location...)." The committed `data/sweep_failures.json` is `[]` (empty), and the current NB02 run reports "0 failures" in its own cell output. The thirteen-table failure this limitation describes belongs to an earlier run that is no longer the one on disk.
3. `REPORT.md` states this account can read "883 tables across 108 databases" with "69,601" columns. The currently committed `data/ground_truth_columns.csv` (which NB03 reads as its ground truth) has **826 tables / 67,575 columns**, and NB02's own freshest run reports "104 databases." `03_semcat_grade.ipynb`'s own saved cell output still displays "ground-truth columns: 69,601 in 870 tables" — a *third* number, from when NB03 was actually executed on 2026-10-02 against an *earlier* version of that same CSV file. In other words, NB03's saved output is already stale relative to the ground-truth file now sitting next to it, independent of any future re-run.

None of this appears to come from fabrication — the WORKLOG's 2026-10-06 entry openly describes adding a new §6a to NB02 and re-running it to give the row-count check notebook provenance (a good instinct, since that number previously lived in an untracked ad-hoc script). What wasn't anticipated is that re-running NB02 top-to-bottom also re-executes its live `DESCRIBE` sweep and SemCat-coverage sections, which read current catalog state and therefore changed, and that `03`/`04` depend on NB02's output files without being re-run in turn. The README's own "Expected drift" paragraph names this exact risk for *future* re-runs, but the drift has already happened once, inside the committed history, and the report text was not reconciled against the final state of its own inputs.

This matters for the project's central methodological claim — that the design is non-circular because ground truth is frozen independently before grading — only in the sense that "frozen" here turned out not to mean "fixed": the ground-truth file changed under the grading notebook without the grading notebook re-running. It is unlikely to overturn the H2 verdict (recall was 32% under two different method passes already, and the coverage numbers are a side computation, not the precision/recall audit itself), but as committed, a reader cannot reproduce `REPORT.md`'s coverage figures by following the README's own instructions, and the claims ledger's "resolved" status on the affected claim is not currently trustworthy on its face.

Limitations are otherwise unusually well handled: the Scope-of-Claim section states the realized cohort (34% of SemCat's indexed universe) against the nominal source and says plainly what the finding is not about; "What Would Have Changed Our Mind" gives the pre-registered refutation route for the main use-case finding and states that H1 failed on two independent routes; and the two numerical corrections made during analysis (precision 77.8%→83.9%; gap median −680.5→−392 aa) are kept visible in `SCOREBOARD.md` rather than quietly overwritten.
